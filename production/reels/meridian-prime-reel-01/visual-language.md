# Visual Language and Continuity

## Constant visual physics

The images may change radically, but they should feel governed by one world:

- crushed blacks
- midnight navy foundations
- cobalt / spectral-blue illumination
- tiny ember-red practical accents
- deep atmospheric haze
- monumental or impossible scale
- humans kept tiny whenever possible
- restrained motion rather than action
- material realism: wet stone, mineral dust, oxidized metal, fog, condensation, cables, membranes
- one uncanny behavior per shot rather than constant spectacle

## Sequence color arc

### Shots I-III: cold exterior / signal world
Cold blue-black, spectral cyan, fog, water, mineral or atmospheric anomalies. Human observers are small and uncertain.

### Shot IV: warm rupture
The first aggressive red/orange interior break. Deep crimson, ember orange, soot, oxidized iron, hematoid quartz, pressure vapor. Minimal cyan. It should feel like crossing an acoustic and thermal boundary.

### Shots V-VI: constructed life / living infrastructure
Retain the red-amber interior family, but move from machine fabrication toward biological ambiguity. The final Worldskin image should feel sacred, enormous, and quietly alive rather than violent.

## Motion rule

A shot should generally contain:

1. a nearly static composition
2. a slow push-in or subtle parallax
3. environmental drift
4. one recognition / pressure / activation cue
5. a hold after the cue
6. hard cut

Avoid fast character movement, action coverage, camera shake, rapid morphing, repeated pulses, clean glossy science fiction, neon spectacle, or obvious monster reveals.
