# Core Visual Thesis

Issue #1 should look like a procedural industrial route comic being swallowed by folklore and old infrastructure memory.

The story is not clean space opera. It is not fantasy quest art. It is not a train story.

The visual grammar is:

```text
heavy tracked machines
cold industrial survival gear
hard black comic shapes
pale blue snow and fog
red signal eyes and relay lights
dirty amber work light
old route equipment that remembers too much
```

The reader should feel that the crew is competent and the world is unfair.

# Issue-Wide Style Lock

## User Reference Lock — Color and Shadows

The six supplied reference crops establish the Issue #1 color and shadow treatment. They are visual study references only: do not paste, trace, collage, or edit them into comic pages. Draw the Traverse crew, vehicles, terrain, relay, and cryptids from this comic's own designs.

The requested Mike Mignola direction means a gothic graphic-comic finish built from deliberate black masses, bold silhouettes, restrained flat color, and selective pools of light. Use the observations below to make the result specific to W.A.S. Traverse rather than reproducing a reference composition or character.

| Reference | Transfer to Issue #1 |
| --- | --- |
| Blue field below a descending black mass | Let black sky, shadow, or machinery swallow much of a panel; cut thin cold-blue seams and vertical marks into it. Save this strongest effect for the relay approach and Page 22. |
| Red figure against muted purple and gray | Use a small, concentrated red signal against broad charcoal, dusty violet, and gray-blue fields. Red marks a warning or focal point, not general fill light. |
| Blue stone and bare branches against pink-violet dusk | Use simplified black terrain silhouettes and limited cool-color planes. A muted lilac horizon can appear in a transitional exterior; it must still feel like Meridian Prime's frozen route. |
| Pale forms emerging from nearly black space | Build threatening shapes from irregular black voids, spare blue-gray contours, and small pale highlights. Keep Jasper physically readable when his action matters. |
| Amber windows inside a black building silhouette | Treat cab windows, work lamps, and the relay housing as isolated dirty-amber rectangles surrounded by black, with little spill. |
| Violet forms with sharp orange and acid-green accents | Reserve rare saturated accent color for a deliberate visual beat; do not introduce green or orange as ambient color across the issue. |

**Shadow rule:** establish the large silhouette first, then add only the contour, seam, or lit tool needed to read the action. Faces may fall partly into black, but hands, sightlines, vehicle direction, the route flags, and the dangerous edge must remain legible. Avoid airbrushed shading, glossy highlights, dense crosshatching, and uniformly detailed surfaces.

**Color hierarchy:** black occupies the most area; cold cobalt, slate blue, and muted violet define terrain and atmosphere; dirty amber identifies human work light; signal red is scarce and narratively loaded. Pale blue is the snow or fog highlight. Maintain clear contrast around dialogue and critical screen information.

**Continuity across the issue:** Pages 1–6 establish black cab interiors and isolated amber/cyan instruments; Pages 7–10 allow red to puncture the cold field around Jasper's warning; Pages 11–17 use broad blue-black terrain and selective GPR light; Pages 18–22 increase black sky mass, amber work-light islands, relay red, and the ink-drip motif. The Page 22 file text must remain readable inside this high-contrast treatment.

## Graphic Mode

Use a heavy-black gothic industrial comic language:

```text
large black shapes
hard-edged silhouettes
flat color fields
crisp ink contours
controlled industrial detail clusters
minimal painterly blending
snow and rock as graphic shapes
strong panel readability at phone size
```

Useful descriptive shorthand:

```text
heavy black spot inks
flat gothic comic shapes
retro animated sci-fi horror color
industrial folklore comic page
hard black silhouettes over cold blue terrain
```

Development shorthand may refer to **Mignola-adjacent black-shape logic**, but production prompts should prefer descriptive language over direct artist-name imitation.

```text
glossy photorealism
soft concept-art haze everywhere
clean superhero rendering
bright white sci-fi lighting
fantasy quest tableau
anime exaggeration
random fake text
visual clutter that kills panel reading
```

# Palette and Lighting Bible

## Main Palette

```text
black sky
blue-black shadows
pale blue snow and fog
cold cobalt distance
dirty amber work lights
red relay beacons
red warning lamps
red machine eyes / sensors
controlled orange cab glow
rare green instrumentation only when needed
```

## White Light Rule

Do not use broad clean white light.

Snow can be bright, but it should read as cold blue, pale blue, or black-edged snow, not clean daylight white.

## Practical Light Sources

Every light should feel motivated:

```text
headlights
cab glow
route monitors
relay beacons
fuel-cache work lamps
red warning nodes
GPR / diagnostic screens
wet black reflection from slush or ice
```

The page should never feel lit by an invisible studio softbox.

# Sky and Atmosphere Lock

The Page 22 sky treatment is now the issue's high-drama sky language.

Use it most strongly on pages 17-22, and more subtly earlier.

Required sky language for relay / exterior climax pages:

```text
deep black upper sky
pale blue cloud bank below
vertical black ink-drips descending into blue
thin blue linework inside black masses
hard negative space
no soft sunset realism
no smooth painterly gradient as primary sky event
```

Interpretation:

```text
The sky looks like ink hanging above the route.
The clouds feel like polar vapor and mineral fog.
The black drips suggest pressure, memory, and bad infrastructure weather.
```

Use this heavily on:

```text
Page 17 - long crawl / relay tower appears
Page 18 - relay tower and fuel cache arrival
Page 19 - mast knocks
Page 20 - relay wake
Page 21 - Rookmask Jack reveal
Page 22 - final W.A.S. file cliffhanger
```

Use it lightly or not at all on interior-heavy early pages.

# Traverse Vehicle and Convoy Lock

## Core Rule

The Traverse is not a train.

It is a crawler convoy.

Use:

```text
tracked tractors
crawler carriers
modular industrial trailers
fuel tanks
fuel bladders
equipment sleds
utility boxes
hitch assemblies
snow tracks
front blades
GPR booms
service rigs
```

Avoid:

```text
rails
railroad tracks
locomotives
passenger cars
station platforms
train windows
railway signage
```

## 409-Type Scout Rig Visual Rule

If a page shows the lead/scout tractor clearly, especially in exterior or GPR contexts, it should evoke a compact Antarctic-style tracked groomer/crawler with crevasse-detection gear.

Required cues:

```text
compact red tracked snow-groomer cab
wide snow tracks
front blade or front chassis assembly
long forward-mounted GPR boom extending ahead of the blade
round / sled-like GPR antenna pod at boom leading end
cold-weather route lights
industrial field modifications
```

Functional logic:

```text
The GPR boom scans hidden crevasses before the crawler's weight crosses the hazard.
The boom gives the crew reaction distance.
The radar operator reads real-time subsurface returns from inside the cab.
```

## Page Application

```text
Pages 1-6: vehicle seen mostly from cab, monitors, mirrors, camera feeds, hitch stress.
Pages 7-10: convoy mass and weight become dangerous during Jasper incident.
Pages 11-16: crawler/GPR logic becomes procedural proof.
Pages 17-22: crawler convoy becomes silhouette and scale at the relay site.
```

# Crew Visual Bible

## Mixed III-Era Crew Rule

The present-day crew must visibly include at least:

```text
Human Rookie Lead Driver
Saurasapien Traverse Lead
Antisapien Signal Tech
Robot Load Tech
```

Do not pause the comic for species exposition. Show the crew through work, silhouette, hands, goggles, posture, and task.

## Wardrobe and Gear Rule

Routine Traverse crews do not default to helmets.

Use:

```text
goggles
route shades
work glasses
collar mics
cold-weather route coats
breather scarves or masks when needed
chest lamps
tool lamps
cab lamps
vehicle lamps
insulated gloves
field harnesses
```

Helmets stay on racks unless the page specifically depicts:

```text
crevasse rescue
rope work
vertical descent
known surface-collapse response
hard-shell hazard operations
```

## Character Separation Rule

The Saurasapien Traverse Lead is not Rookmask Jack.

Never give the Traverse Lead:

```text
red glowing eye
bird beak
black hood
plague mask
relay staff
Jack silhouette
servo-still machine posture
```

The Traverse Lead is a living saurian person in route gear. Jack is a black-alloy machine-body witness wearing a bird-beak mask.

# Rookmask Jack Visual Bible

## Core Read

```text
Rookmask Jack is a black-alloy humanoid robot wearing tattered black robes and a hard black bird-beak mask.
The beak is equipment, not anatomy.
The red eye is a lens, sensor, or machine glow, not an animal eye.
```

## Profile Rule

When Jack is in profile, show **one visible red eye only**.

Do not show a second eye through the far side of the mask.

## Required Details

```text
hard black bird-beak mask
one dim red eye in profile
hood swallowing skull line
black robe mass
black alloy robot hands
black mechanical feet
old relay antenna used as staff
cables, dish fragments, or signal hardware on staff
servo-still posture
```

## Forbidden Jack Drift

Do not let Jack become:

```text
organic bird creature
bird-person
feathered humanoid
animal monster
fantasy plague doctor
wizard
superhero villain
clean robot
normal person in costume
```

Feather-like shapes are allowed only when they come from:

```text
robe shreds
cables
mask ridges
black silhouette tears
weathered fabric edges
```

They must not read as organic plumage.

## Story Discipline

Issue #1 may hint that Jack is a machine-body / infrastructure-body witness.

It must not explain:

```text
Mark Aggressive never fully left the Core
Jack is what Mark can still reach
Rookmask's infrastructure logic
C.A.S.E.
```

Those answers belong later.

Jasper is the first creature shock, but he is not an attacker.

Visual function:

```text

```

Cues:

```text
human-sized shaggy figure
lynx-eared silhouette
round goggles
scavenged route gear
salvage straps
red headlamp
scrap-metal tool
small cat-shaped charm, tiny and unexplained
```

Avoid:

```text
werewolf attack pose
big monster charge
blood or gore
cute mascot look
full Buck reveal
```

The reader should understand after Page 9 that Jasper was stopping the crew, not hunting them.
