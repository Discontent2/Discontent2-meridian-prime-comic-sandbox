# Core Revision Decisions

## 1. Traverse Helmet / Goggles Rule

Traverse crews do not normally wear helmets during routine travel, cab operation, load checks, or refueling.

Most of the time they wear:

```text
goggles
route shades
work glasses
collar mics
cold-weather field gear
breather scarves or masks when needed
headlamps or work lamps mounted to clothing, harnesses, tractors, or tools
```

Helmets are reserved for:

```text
crevasse training
crevasse rescue
rope work
vertical descent
known surface-collapse response
severe exposure operations where hard-shell protection is required
```

### Script Impact

Replace generic helmet language in Issue #1 v0.2 with goggles, shades, work lamps, and field gear unless a scene specifically requires rescue or crevasse protocol.

Pages most affected:

```text
Page 1: Rookie in cab, goggles/shades available but helmet off.
Page 12: load check uses goggles/work lamps, not helmets.
Pages 18-21: refueling uses goggles/work lamps, not helmets.
Page 22: reflections should use cab glass, goggles, and screens unless a specific helmet is justified.
```

This keeps Traverse crews visually grounded as professional route workers rather than generic astronauts.

## 2. III-Era Mixed Traverse Crew Rule

Traverse teams in Tenet Nocsid III's era are mixed-species, multi-culture working crews.

Because Traverse teams regularly travel between Prime Ops, Hydropolis, and the Craton, a standard III-era Traverse crew should include at least:

```text
one human
one robot
one Saurasapien
one Antisapien
```

This should shape Issue #1 immediately because the present-day crew belongs to the III-era working world even if the W.A.S. disaster itself is older.

### Recommended Issue #1 Functional Assignments

```text
Rookie Lead Driver: human
Traverse Lead: Saurasapien
Cab Tech / Signal Tech: Antisapien
Load Tech or Fuel Tech: robot
Rear Driver: human or Saurasapien, to be decided in v0.2
```

### Voice Logic

```text
Human Rookie: shame, fatigue, tries to sound calmer than he is.
Saurasapien Traverse Lead: disciplined route authority, short commands, physical steadiness.
Antisapien Cab/Signal Tech: suspicious of clean signals, notices route-network anomalies first.
Robot Load/Fuel Tech: procedural honesty, blunt diagnostics, useful pressure relief.
```

### Script Impact

Issue #1 v0.2 should keep the incident readable without stopping for character exposition. Species should appear through silhouettes, speech patterns, goggles/shades, cab placement, hands, posture, and work roles.

Do not pause the wrong-route survival story to explain species politics.

## 3. Rookmask Jack Infrastructure-Body Revision

Rookmask Jack should move away from a simple physical Mark Aggressive transformation.

New preferred direction:

```text
Mark Aggressive never fully left the Core.
Rookmask Jack is a route-service machine, robot, relay body, or WISSARD infrastructure body that Mark Aggressive can still reach.
```

Core principle:

```text
Rookmask Jack is not Mark Aggressive's body.
Rookmask Jack is what Mark Aggressive can still reach.
```

This keeps Mark's tragedy tied to WISSARD access, route infrastructure, guilt, and the Core.

### Working Interpretation

```text
Buck came back wrong as Jasper.
Richard came back wrong as X.
Mara came back wrong as Sparky.
Flagstaff came back wrong as damaged route logic.
Mark did not come back cleanly.

Mark remained trapped in, or entangled with, the Core.
His WISSARD credentials, guilt, authority, and signal access survived in infrastructure.
Rookmask Jack is a machine body or route-service shell operated by that trapped signal.
```

### Issue #1 Reveal Discipline

Do not explain this in Issue #1.

Issue #1 may show only visual and behavioral clues:

```text
Jack stands too still.
Jack moves with servo stiffness.
A metal foot or mechanical limb is visible under the robe.
The cowl may be a service housing, not a face.
The crooked staff may plug into, key, or authenticate with the relay mast.
The relay responds to Jack as if he has valid credentials.
Jack is reflected as machine over the old Mark Aggressive personnel file.
```

The reader should ask:

```text
Why does that machine have a personnel file?
```

The answer belongs later, not in Issue #1.
