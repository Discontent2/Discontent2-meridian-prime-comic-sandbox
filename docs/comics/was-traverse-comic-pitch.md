# Pitch Document

## High Concept / Logline

The first WISSARD team sent into Meridian Prime's Core returns changed into the cryptids future travelers fear, pity, and quietly rely on. As WISSARD tries to erase the failed mission and recover its "assets," the broken survivors must decide whether they are still a crew, still people, or only warnings left along a road that should never be opened.

---

## Thematic Core

**What survives when duty destroys identity?**

The arc debates two kinds of loyalty:

```text
Institutional loyalty:
Complete the mission. Obey the route. Preserve the data.

Living loyalty:
Protect the crew. Warn the next traveler. Refuse the road when the road is wrong.
```

The emotional engine is not only "humans become monsters." It is sharper and sadder:

```text
People become folklore because no institution will admit what happened to them.
```

The horror is not transformation alone. It is abandonment, bad maps, obsolete orders, and the ache of being remembered incorrectly.

---

## Character Arcs

### Mara "Sparky" Cairn / The Cairn Hound

**Starts:** The W.A.S. Traverse mountaineer, practical and emotionally guarded. She trusts terrain, rope, judgment, and procedure more than sentiment.

**Tested by:** The Core meshes her with the forgotten memory of Massif "Massey," the dog she left behind. Her survival instinct becomes protective, loyal, and territorial.

**Ends:** She accepts that she is no longer the person who left, but she is still someone who can protect others. She rejects WISSARD's recovery order and becomes a guardian of impossible routes.

### Mark Aggressive / Rookmask Jack

**Starts:** WISSARD-appointed Lead Science Officer, brilliant and driven, convinced that entering the Core before it is ready is justified by historic necessity.

**Tested by:** His childhood memory of birds at his mother's funeral fuses with his guilt and authority. He becomes a watcher, omen, and witness rather than a leader.

**Ends:** He sends a request for help he knows will not be answered. His tragedy is that he finally understands warning, but only after leading everyone past the last safe point.

### Richard Jameson / X

**Starts:** Lead Operator, machine-minded, controlled, and loyal to systems because systems make sense.

**Tested by:** The Core drags up the isopods he kept with his father, fusing childhood tenderness with WISSARD procedure-horror.

**Ends:** He stops trying to restore the system and instead learns to break the right parts of it. His first true act as X is sabotage in defense of the crew.

### "Buck" / Ol' Jasper

**Starts:** Traverse mechanic, big-hearted, underestimated, brilliant with machines but emotionally simple in the best way. His cat back at Prime Ops is his private anchor.

**Tested by:** The Core makes him frightening, shaggy, gentle, and misunderstood. His attempts to help look like false signals.

**Ends:** He saves the team by scaring them away from danger, establishing the folklore pattern future travelers will later misunderstand.

### Flagstaff / The Last Surveyor

**Starts:** The W.A.S. Traverse guide drone, built to mark safe routes and trust map logic.

**Tested by:** The Core damages his route systems. His loyalty remains, but the world no longer matches his map.

**Ends:** He becomes the tragic infrastructure ghost of the arc: still helping, still wrong, still useful if understood.

## Stakes

### Personal

If the team fails, they stop being a crew and become isolated monsters hunted, studied, or erased. Mara loses the last chance to choose what Sparky means. Mark's mistake becomes the only story anyone tells about them.

### Local

The old Core route remains open. Future Traverse teams, salvage crews, and travelers will follow bad flags, false lights, WISSARD equipment pings, and Core-contaminated maps into death.

### Global / Institutional

WISSARD can classify the incident as a success, weaponize the survivors as proof of survivable Core transit, and send more teams through before anyone understands the cost. Meridian Prime gains not one failed expedition, but a repeating machine for making cryptids out of people.

# 5-Issue Arc Structure

## Issue 1 - The Red Light Walks

**Function:** Hook / Inciting Incident  
**Act Position:** Act 1  
**Monthly Promise:** A survival horror encounter that becomes a mystery.

### A-Plot

A present-day Traverse crew follows a red light toward a broken route and nearly falls into a crevasse. Ol' Jasper terrifies them away from the edge. Nearby, a black flag line contradicts GPS. A dead relay mast begins transmitting an old WISSARD emergency packet.

### B-Plot

We glimpse the old W.A.S. Traverse team before the Core: Mark in command, Mara skeptical, Richard preparing systems, Buck joking with machines, Flagstaff marking the route.

### C-Plot

A Hydropolis archive tag mentions "Core-returned remnant folklore" alongside unrelated cryptids, implying the pattern is hidden among ordinary monster stories.

### Issue Value

Begins with a survival set piece, introduces Jasper and Flagstaff as scary-but-protective hazards, and gives readers a complete "wrong route" incident.

### Page 22 Cliffhanger

The decoded relay packet displays a damaged crew image:

```text
The "monsters" have WISSARD personnel names.
Mark Aggressive is listed as team lead.
```

---

## Issue 2 - The Core Was Not Ready

**Function:** Escalation / World-building  
**Act Position:** Act 2A  
**Monthly Promise:** A doomed expedition issue.

### A-Plot

Flashback issue centered on the W.A.S. Traverse before entry. WISSARD pushes the mission forward despite route anomalies. Mara notices terrain behavior that makes no mountaineering sense. Flagstaff's route flags briefly disagree with themselves.

### B-Plot

The crew bonds. Buck humanizes the team. Richard's father/isopod memory appears in small, quiet beats. Mara's dog Massey appears only through memory and objects, not melodrama.

### C-Plot

WISSARD command insists the Core is a transit problem, not a consent problem.

### Issue Value

Readers get the team as people before the cryptid forms, making the tragedy land harder.

### Page 22 Cliffhanger

Mark overrules Mara's abort call. The Core opens. The team steps through. Flagstaff's map updates to a route that does not exist.

---

## Issue 3 - The Shape Memory of Water and Stone

**Function:** Midpoint Shift  
**Act Position:** Act 2B  
**Monthly Promise:** A Core transformation nightmare.

### A-Plot

Inside the Core, navigation fails. The environment behaves like memory wearing geology as a mask. Each team member encounters the forgotten thing that will reshape them: birds, isopods, a cat, Massey, and Flagstaff's dead route logic.

### B-Plot

Mara becomes the emotional center. Her flaw breaks: she cannot solve this with rope, rank, or route judgment. She has to admit fear, attachment, and dependence.

### C-Plot

A side sequence cuts to a Hydropolis archive specimen, the Eustatic Devil, reminding readers not every cryptid is W.A.S. related. This keeps the mystery textured rather than overly neat.

### Midpoint Turn

The team escapes the Core, but the escape is not success. They return changed.

### Page 22 Cliffhanger

The returned team sees their reflections in wet black stone: not quite human, not quite animal, not metaphor.

```text
They are already becoming the stories.
```

---

## Issue 4 - Still Us For Now

**Function:** Dark Night / Lowest Point  
**Act Position:** Act 2B into Act 3 setup  
**Monthly Promise:** A broken-team tragedy.

### A-Plot

WISSARD recovery protocol activates. The institution does not send rescue. It sends containment. The team hides in a field camp while their bodies and instincts worsen. Mark records a request for help he knows will be denied.

### B-Plot

The last crew picture happens here. X, Jasper, Sparky, and Flagstaff are still together. Jack is already pulling away, becoming witness more than teammate.

### C-Plot

WISSARD rewrites the incident as equipment loss and route instability. The first official lies are born.

### Emotional Break

Mark admits he sent them in too early. Mara refuses to let his guilt define what remains of them.

### Page 22 Cliffhanger

WISSARD drones arrive. Mark stays behind at the relay mast to mislead them. The others flee. The team fractures.

---

## Issue 5 - The Road That Came Back Wrong

**Function:** Climax / New Status Quo  
**Act Position:** Act 3  
**Monthly Promise:** Cryptid-remnant climax and new folklore status quo.

### A-Plot

Present and past collide. The current Traverse crew, following the old emergency packet, reaches the same Core-adjacent route. WISSARD salvage units try to recover data and specimens. The cryptid remnants intervene, not as superheroes, but as warnings made flesh.

### B-Plot

Mara/Sparky chooses her new identity. She is not rescued, cured, or reclaimed. She becomes guardian by choice. X destroys the Core-interface machinery. Jasper drives the current crew away from the false safe route. Flagstaff plants the wrong flag, then hesitates when Sparky blocks him. For one beat, obsolete map and living judgment meet.

### C-Plot

Rookmask Jack watches from the relay line. He does not rejoin. He knocks the mast: warning, farewell, confession.

### Climax

The Core route is sealed or rendered unusable, not by WISSARD command, but by the remnants refusing to let the road be used again.

### Resolution

The present-day Traverse crew survives and later tells the story wrong, but warmly. The cryptids vanish back into their territories. Hydropolis remains in the distance, carrying the next arc seed.

### Final Page

A new black file opens at WISSARD:

```text
W.A.S. TRAVERSE: PERSONNEL STATUS - UNRESOLVED
```

A second file beneath it reads:

```text
CORE TRANSIT: PHASE TWO AUTHORIZED
```

# Serialized Pacing Mechanics

## A-Plot

What happened to the W.A.S. Traverse, and can the remnants stop WISSARD from repeating it?

## B-Plot

Mara's arc from disciplined survivor to chosen guardian gives the trade its emotional spine. Mark's guilt runs opposite her: he becomes warning because he failed to heed warning.

## C-Plot

The Eustatic Devil and Hydropolis materials imply a larger cryptid ecology beyond W.A.S. Traverse. Not every monster is a Core-returned person. That matters because it keeps Meridian Prime from feeling like a single mystery box.

## Monthly Issue Promise

```text
Issue 1: survival horror encounter
Issue 2: doomed expedition setup
Issue 3: Core transformation nightmare
Issue 4: broken-team tragedy
Issue 5: cryptid-remnant climax and new folklore status quo
```

## Trade Paperback Shape

Collected, the arc reads as a single tragedy:

```text
A team enters the Core too early.
The Core returns them as warnings.
The institution abandons them.
The warnings save someone else.
```

# Visual Tone Guidance

Carry forward the locked reel language for any comic covers, pitch art, trailer panels, or issue interiors:

- bad 1980s sci-fi movie energy
- no white light
- colored source lighting
- wet reflections
- saturated artificial light
- lonely silhouettes
- practical-effects mood
- future traveler stories
- fear softening into recognition

The comic should feel like a longer narrative version of the reel's emotional grammar:

```text
wonder -> isolation -> strange encounter -> unease -> recognition -> tenderness -> unresolved distance
```
