## Purpose

This beat sheet is a page-budgeting and page-rhythm tool for Issue #1 before full script drafting.

It bridges the high-level W.A.S. Traverse comic pitch and the eventual full script by assigning story function to all 22 pages, protecting page-turn reveals, clarifying Traverse operating procedure, and preventing scene bloat.

Core issue promise:

```text
A present-day Traverse crew drifts off the approved route after a rookie blindly follows obsolete physical flags, then survives Jasper's terrifying intervention, discovers a parallel subsurface crevasse, and reaches an old relay tower where Rookmask Jack unlocks the first buried W.A.S. Traverse file.
```

---

## Operational Premise Lock

Traverse crews rarely leave their equipment while underway.

Most of Issue #1 should be staged through:

```text
fogged windshield
headlight cones
radio transmission
rear tractor feeds
side mirrors
dash monitors
route GPS
GPR display
cab glass reflections
wiper streaks
helmet lights during required exterior operations
```

Crew exit is limited to operational necessity:

```text
load check after stop-ops
refueling at the relay tower / fuel cache
emergency inspection or repair
```

No casual hiking. No wandering around because someone is curious. The world outside the Traverse train should feel hostile, partially unreadable, and expensive to enter.

---

## Issue Spine

```text
A rookie lead driver blindly follows obsolete physical flags while the Traverse Lead is in the galley module writing the daily SITREP. By the time the rookie realizes they are off course, shame makes him hide the mistake. A red light appears near the approved route, promising an unnoticed correction. But Jasper forces the Traverse train off that correction, revealing the approved route is paralleled by a hidden subsurface crevasse. The crew pushes toward an old relay tower and fuel cache, where Rookmask Jack is waiting.
```

---

## Splash Budget

```text
Page 1: near-splash / big cinematic opener through lead tractor windshield
Page 22: full-page cliffhanger splash
```

Page 22 gets the true money shot.

Page 1 can be a mostly silent, large-panel opener without spending a full splash if page economy needs tightening.

# 22-Page Page-by-Page Breakdown

## Page 1 - The Lead Tractor Sees the Road

**Panel Budget:** 3-4 panels  
**Function:** Atmospheric opener  
**Primary POV:** Inside lead tractor cab  

### Beat

Fogged windshield. Headlights tunnel through black-blue mineral mist. The Traverse train crawls across wet frozen flats, following a neat line of black route flags.

Dashboard glow. Wiper streaks. Condensation. The rookie lead driver watches the flags more than the route monitor.

### Key Image

```text
Physical flags in the headlights feel more real than the approved GPS route.
```

### End Beat

The approved route display shows a slight disagreement, but the rookie does not notice.

---

## Page 2 - Galley SITREP

**Panel Budget:** 5-6 panels  
**Function:** Establish command structure and routine procedure  
**Primary POV:** Galley module / radio network  

### Beat

The Traverse Lead is in the galley module generating the day's SITREP: fuel, load strain, weather, route status, crew condition.

Other crew are distributed through tractors and modules, speaking over radio. The run feels routine, disciplined, and procedural.

### Purpose

Show why the rookie has the lead momentarily, without making the Traverse Lead incompetent.

### End Beat

Lead asks for a routine route confirmation. Rookie answers too casually:

```text
Flags are clean. Holding course.
```

---

## Page 3 - Odd-Page Reveal: The Old Survey Drone

**Panel Budget:** 4-5 panels  
**Function:** First page-turn unease / Flagstaff seed  
**Primary POV:** Rear tractor windshield / side camera feed  

### Beat

A rear tractor crew member spots something off the flank: an old yellow-white survey drone half-shrouded in fog, near the flag line they just passed.

Tarp scraps. Broken antenna. Old route-service chassis.

Rear crew radios in:

```text
Was that a survey drone?
```

Another crew member answers:

```text
Late model.
```

### Odd-Page Reveal

```text
Flagstaff / The Flagger has already been near the route.
```

### End Beat

In the lead cab, the rookie finally glances at the GPS.

---

## Page 4 - The Rookie Notices

**Panel Budget:** 5 panels  
**Function:** Human error enters the machine  
**Primary POV:** Lead tractor cab  

### Beat

The rookie realizes the Traverse train is off course. Not catastrophically, but enough. The approved route is bending away behind fog and black flats. The physical flags are leading them along a dead line.

He almost keys the radio.

He stops.

He looks toward the galley channel. The Traverse Lead is still speaking SITREP details.

### Emotional Beat

```text
Shame enters the cab.
```

### End Beat

The rookie decides he can correct quietly.

---

## Page 5 - The Red Light

**Panel Budget:** 4-5 panels  
**Function:** Temptation / false correction  
**Primary POV:** Lead windshield, distorted by fog and headlight bloom  

### Beat

A red light appears in the distance, roughly in the direction of the current approved route. It looks like a marker, beacon, or another crew light.

The rookie sees a way to correct course without admitting the mistake.

### Important Visual Note

The red light should be ambiguous:

```text
It could be safety.
It could be a moving creature.
It could be both.
```

### End Beat

The rookie begins steering toward it.

---

## Page 6 - Quiet Course Correction

**Panel Budget:** 5-6 panels  
**Function:** Hidden error escalates into train-wide risk  
**Primary POV:** Lead cab / rear tractor radio / module strain monitors  

### Beat

The rookie eases the lead tractor toward the red light. The Traverse train follows. Hitches flex. Load monitors tick upward.

Rear tractor asks if they are shifting route. Rookie says it is a minor correction around rough ground.

The Traverse Lead half-hears this over radio but does not yet detect the scale of the problem.

### End Beat

The red light moves.

---

## Page 7 - Odd-Page Reveal: Jasper Starts Running

**Panel Budget:** 1 large reveal + 2-3 smaller panels  
**Function:** First cryptid reveal / false threat  
**Primary POV:** Lead windshield and side mirror  

### Beat

Ol' Jasper appears in the headlights and fog, red headlamp blazing. Shaggy body. Lynx ears. Salvage gear. Goggles.

He is running parallel to the Traverse train, not quite attacking, not quite fleeing.

### Odd-Page Reveal

```text
The “safe” red light is attached to something alive.
```

### End Beat

Jasper cuts across the lead tractor's light cone, forcing the rookie to react.

---

## Page 8 - The Bad Correction Gets Worse

**Panel Budget:** 5-6 panels  
**Function:** Operational panic  
**Primary POV:** Cab chaos, radio chatter, rear camera feeds  

### Beat

The rookie panics and hard-corrects.

Not a clean turn. Not a full reverse. A dangerous steering correction with a heavy load behind him.

Rear tractor shouts over radio. Hitch strain alarms spike. Module interiors lurch.

### Operational Danger

```text
A hard reverse or abrupt correction could jackknife the Traverse train.
```

### End Beat

The Traverse train deviates again, now farther from the approved route.

---

## Page 9 - Odd-Page Reveal: Jasper Was Herding Them

**Panel Budget:** 1 large windshield reveal + 2-3 smaller panels  
**Function:** Monster reframed as damaged warning  
**Primary POV:** Headlights catching the terrain Jasper blocked  

### Beat

Jasper flashes, slams scrap, or throws himself into the light path one last time.

The headlights catch the reason: the ground where the rookie was steering looks smooth, but a hairline fracture opens under the surface crust.

### Odd-Page Reveal

```text
Jasper was not chasing them for prey.
He was stopping the correction.
```

### End Beat

The rookie realizes the “safe” direction was not safe.

---

## Page 10 - The Traverse Lead Takes Command

**Panel Budget:** 5 panels  
**Function:** Authority snaps into place  
**Primary POV:** Galley module command channel / lead cab / rear tractor  

### Beat

The Traverse Lead hears enough chaos to know something is wrong. He cuts through the radio chatter and orders everyone to hold formation and continue straight until they reach a stable stop point.

No reversing.

No improvising.

No one exits yet.

The rookie nearly confesses, but the radio channel is too busy.

### End Beat

Traverse Lead issues the phrase everyone obeys:

```text
Stop ops in one mile.
```

---

## Page 11 - Stop Ops

**Panel Budget:** 4-5 panels  
**Function:** Controlled halt after chaos  
**Primary POV:** Exterior wide shot, then cab/module interiors  

### Beat

The Traverse train comes to a controlled stop on a stable stretch. Headlights burn through fog. Rear tractors brake. Modules settle.

The silence after alarms feels heavy.

The Traverse Lead gets on all-channel radio.

### Command

```text
Check loads.
Check hitches.
Check couplings.
Nobody wanders.
```

### End Beat

The rookie sits in the lead cab, staring at the off-course route monitor.

---

## Page 12 - Load Check

**Panel Budget:** 5-6 panels  
**Function:** Necessary exterior operation / consequences visible  
**Primary POV:** Operational exterior, close to equipment  

### Beat

Crew exit only now, because the load check requires it. Suited figures move through headlights, fog, and wet reflections.

They inspect couplings, sledges, module locks, fuel lines, and hitch strain. Communication stays on radio.

### Important Staging

```text
No casual wandering.
They stay close to equipment.
```

### End Beat

The Traverse Lead sees the flag line, the GPS drift, and the rookie's silence. He knows enough.

---

## Page 13 - Odd-Page Reveal: The GPR Tractor

**Panel Budget:** 1 larger panel + 3 smaller operational panels  
**Function:** Tool reveal / route recovery plan  
**Primary POV:** Equipment bay / side ramp  

### Beat

The Traverse Lead unstows the GPR tractor: a smaller scout vehicle with ground-penetrating radar, low lights, sensor rig, and hazard-mapping equipment.

It drops from the equipment rack like a little beetle built for bad decisions.

### Odd-Page Reveal

```text
They have a tool for finding roads beneath roads.
```

### End Beat

Lead tells the train:

```text
Follow my track at safe distance. Nobody freelances.
```

---

## Page 14 - Scouting Back Toward the Route

**Panel Budget:** 5-6 panels  
**Function:** Procedure against terrain uncertainty  
**Primary POV:** GPR tractor cab / lead train following at distance  

### Beat

The Traverse Lead drives the GPR tractor ahead, probing for a safe path back to the approved route.

The Traverse train follows slowly behind. The rookie remains in lead tractor, now under radio silence except for acknowledgments.

GPR returns strange void signatures under smooth ground.

### End Beat

The approved route appears close on GPS, but the GPR says something is between them and it.

---

## Page 15 - Odd-Page Reveal: The Crevasse Runs Parallel

**Panel Budget:** 1 large map/sensor reveal + 2 small reaction panels  
**Function:** Route trap reveal  
**Primary POV:** GPR display and windshield  

### Beat

The subsurface crevasse is not a single crack to cross.

It runs parallel to the approved route, shadowing it like a buried river of empty space.

### Odd-Page Reveal

```text
The safe route is nearby, but unreachable.
```

### End Beat

Turning around is dangerous. Crossing blind is worse.

---

## Page 16 - The Relay Tower Option

**Panel Budget:** 5 panels  
**Function:** Competent least-bad decision  
**Primary POV:** GPR tractor radio / galley map / lead cab monitor  

### Beat

The Traverse Lead finds an old option: relay tower and fuel cache, twenty-nine miles ahead.

Old records show a bridged path near the tower that may connect back to the approved route.

He chooses forward, not because forward is safe, but because every other option is worse.

### Important Note

```text
This is a competent decision under bad information, not reckless heroics.
```

### End Beat

The rookie understands his mistake has become a twenty-nine-mile problem.

---

## Page 17 - The Long Crawl

**Panel Budget:** 4-5 panels  
**Function:** Atmospheric travel / pressure reset before final location  
**Primary POV:** Multiple equipment views  

### Beat

The Traverse train crawls through fog behind the GPR tractor.

Headlights, rear lights, radio static, module interiors, tense crew faces.

The old flag line disappears behind them.

Somewhere far back, the late-model survey drone may be glimpsed only as a tiny amber dot in a rear camera, still working.

### End Beat

A dead relay tower appears on the horizon.

---

## Page 18 - Relay Tower and Fuel Cache

**Panel Budget:** 5-6 panels  
**Function:** Arrival / refuel setup  
**Primary POV:** Exterior wide, then operational close-ups  

### Beat

The relay tower rises from black frost beside an old fuel cache.

Rusted tanks. Dead mast. Frozen cables. Ancient warning plates. The place feels abandoned but useful.

The Traverse Lead orders refuel, load check, and minimal perimeter.

Crew exit because refueling is necessary.

### End Beat

While refueling starts, the relay mast gives a single metallic knock.

---

## Page 19 - Something at the Mast

**Panel Budget:** 5 panels  
**Function:** Refueling unease / false Jasper echo  
**Primary POV:** Helmet lights, vehicle headlights, fuel operation, radio  

### Beat

The crew freezes. They are outside now, but tethered to procedure. Fuel lines remain attached.

One crew member thinks a cable shifted. Another sees a shape near the mast, just beyond the headlight bloom.

The rookie sees red in the fog and thinks Jasper followed them.

It is not Jasper.

### End Beat

Another knock travels down the relay tower.

---

## Page 20 - Relay Wake

**Panel Budget:** 5-6 panels  
**Function:** WISSARD mystery activates  
**Primary POV:** Fuel cache terminal / cab screens / relay mast  

### Beat

The relay tower, supposedly dead, begins waking in fragments.

Old WISSARD signal handshake. Static. Corrupted emergency packet.

A crew member in the cab radios that something is opening on the system.

The Traverse Lead orders everyone to keep refueling but not touch the mast.

### End Beat

The relay lights catch a hooded silhouette.

---

## Page 21 - Odd-Page Reveal: Rookmask Jack

**Panel Budget:** 1 large reveal + 2 small reaction panels  
**Function:** Final cryptid reveal before splash  
**Primary POV:** Headlights and relay glow  

### Beat

Rookmask Jack stands at the relay mast.

Hooded black robes. Birdlike plague-mask cowl. Crooked staff. Old Traverse backpack.

He is not attacking.

He is watching the refuel operation and the signal waking beneath it.

### Odd-Page Reveal

```text
The relay tower has a witness.
```

Jack taps the mast once with his staff.

### End Beat

Every screen in the Traverse train flickers at once.

---

## Page 22 - Final Splash: The Old File Opens

**Panel Budget:** Full-page splash  
**Function:** Commercial cliffhanger / arc door opens  
**Primary POV:** Composite through fogged windshield / relay screen / helmet reflection  

### Beat

Full-page cliffhanger. Rookmask Jack is outside by the relay mast, reflected in cab glass and visor glare.

On the awakened relay display, an old WISSARD file opens.

File header:

```text
W.A.S. TRAVERSE
CORE TRANSIT INCIDENT
PERSONNEL STATUS: UNRESOLVED
```

A corrupted ID fragment resolves just enough:

```text
MARK AGGRESSIVE
TRAVERSE LEADER / LEAD SCIENCE OFFICER
```

Final line appears underneath:

```text
PHASE TWO AUTHORIZED
```

### Cliffhanger

The crew came for fuel and a way back to the route.

They found the first buried proof that the road has done this before, and WISSARD may be preparing to do it again.

# Issue #1 A/B/C Plot Weaving

## A-Plot

A present-day Traverse crew drifts off route, survives a wrong-route hazard, and reaches an old relay tower/fuel cache after discovering that the approved route is paralleled by a hidden subsurface crevasse.

## B-Plot

The cryptids and route remnants initially look like threats, but each is tied to some damaged form of warning: Flagstaff through obsolete flags, Jasper through frightening intervention, Jack through witness behavior at the relay mast.

## C-Plot

The dead relay tower reveals WISSARD buried the truth of the first Core transit incident and may be preparing another phase.
