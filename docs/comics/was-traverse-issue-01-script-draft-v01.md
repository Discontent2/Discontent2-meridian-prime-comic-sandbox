## Script Intent

This script turns the Issue #1 beat sheet and hybrid architecture pass into a full page-by-page comic script.

Core rule:

```text
The present-day crew lives the incident.
The W.A.S. crew haunts the evidence.
```

The issue remains a present-day Traverse survival horror story. The W.A.S. layer appears through corrupted equipment behavior, route memory, old telemetry, ghosted reflections, damaged warning patterns, and buried institutional signal.

Do not treat W.A.S. as fully explained until Page 22.

Do not reveal C.A.S.E. in Issue #1.

---

## Production Guardrails

```text
No casual exterior wandering.
Crew exits only for procedure: load check and refuel.
Cryptids appear as warnings before explanations.
Page 22 reveals W.A.S., not the whole backstory.
C.A.S.E. remains buried for a later issue or file reveal.
Rookie is tired, pressured, and ashamed, not stupid.
Traverse Lead is competent, not absent-minded.
The machinery is a POV system, not background dressing.
```

**Panel Count:** 4  
**Page Function:** Atmospheric opener / route problem seed  
**Primary POV:** Inside lead tractor cab  

### Panel 1

Large horizontal opener. Interior of the lead tractor cab, looking out through a fogged windshield. Headlights tunnel into black-blue mineral mist. Wet frozen flats glitter under the light. A neat line of black route flags leads forward into the dark.

**CAPTION:**  
The approved route was clean.

### Panel 2

Closer inside the cab. The ROOKIE LEAD DRIVER, suited but helmet off, watches the flags with tired focus. Dashboard glow paints his face cyan and amber. Wiper streaks cut the glass. Condensation gathers at the edges.

**CAPTION:**  
Clean flags. Clean weather window. Clean convoy status.

### Panel 3

Close on the route monitor. The approved GPS route line bends slightly left. The physical flags seen through the windshield continue straight.

No one is looking at the monitor.

**SFX:**  
tik  
tik  
tik

### Panel 4

Tight reflection in the windshield. For one instant, the cab reflection looks older than the cab itself: a different dash shape, a different seat silhouette, a different route label underneath the modern display. It is too faint to read.

**CAPTION:**  
Clean lies best.

# Lettering and SFX Notes

## Lettering Tone

The issue should use lettering as an operational instrument. Radio dialogue should feel clipped, procedural, and pressure-tested. Captions should be sparse and cold, more like field notes than narration.

Preferred lettering modes:

```text
CAPTION - minimal, atmospheric, field-file voice
RADIO - hard-edged balloon or radio-tail treatment
SYSTEM TEXT - small, distorted, used sparingly
SFX - physical, metal, frost, static, not cartoonish
```

Avoid over-lettering the panels. The issue depends on silence, breath, headlight space, and unreadable distance.

## Key SFX Palette

```text
tik - dashboard or route monitor tick
KRRRNNK - hitch strain
SKRRRNNNN - heavy train correction / sled friction
KLANG - Jasper striking scrap or metal tool
KRAK - hidden surface fracture
HSSSSSS - controlled stop / brakes / pressure bleed
KTHUNK - equipment bay ramp
HISSS - steam / hydraulics
KONK - relay mast knock
TOK - Jack's staff tap
zzzt - relay terminal waking
```

# Character and Role List for This Issue

## Present-Day Crew

### Traverse Lead

The competent operator-in-command for the present-day incident. He is not fooled by the world for long, but the route problem begins while he is physically displaced into the galley module for SITREP work. His authority lands on Page 10.

### Rookie Lead Driver

The human error vector. He trusts physical flags, misses the route monitor disagreement, then hides the mistake out of shame. He is tired, pressured, and afraid of reporting a correctable error that becomes uncorrectable.

### Rear Driver

Rear tractor operator. First to spot the old survey drone, first to feel the convoy strain from behind.

### Load Tech

Monitors hitches, couplings, strain, and module stability. Provides practical, grim operational texture.

### Fuel Tech

Handles fuel reserve, bladder stability, and refuel operations at the relay tower. Gives the relay-tower sequence procedure and vulnerability.

### Cab Tech

Monitors system handshakes and old signal behavior during the relay wake sequence.

# Cryptid and Remnant Usage

## Flagstaff / The Last Surveyor

Appears first as damaged route infrastructure. Should not be fully explained. The old survey drone is a warning seed, not a monster reveal.

Appears as a false threat on Page 7, then is reframed as a warning on Page 9. His cat charm is a tender visual shard, not an explained clue yet.

## Rookmask Jack

Appears at the relay mast on Page 21. He does not attack. He witnesses, activates, and warns. His identity is not spoken, but Page 22 connects Mark Aggressive to the W.A.S. file.
