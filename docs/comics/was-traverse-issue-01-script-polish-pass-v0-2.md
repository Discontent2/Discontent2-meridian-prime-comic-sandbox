## Purpose

This document performs the first professional polish pass on `was-traverse-issue-01-script-v0-1.md` before any finished art is created.

The v0.1 script is structurally sound: it follows the present-day wrong-route incident, keeps W.A.S. bleedthrough subtle, saves the readable W.A.S. confirmation for Page 22, and keeps C.A.S.E. out of Issue #1.

This polish pass identifies what should be tightened before thumbnails and page production.

---

## Overall Diagnosis

```text
v0.1 is ready for polish, not art.
```

The script has the right spine:

```text
flags -> mistake -> red light -> Jasper -> hidden fracture -> stop ops -> GPR -> parallel crevasse -> relay tower -> Jack -> W.A.S. file
```

The main v0.2 need is not new plot. The main need is production clarity:

```text
name the functional crew roles cleanly
reduce captions where panels already carry meaning
protect lettering space
make radio voices more distinct
clarify exact exterior-operation boundaries
keep W.A.S. clues suggestive until Page 22
```

---

# Global Polish Decisions

## 1. Name only what production needs

v0.1 uses functional crew labels: Rookie, Traverse Lead, Rear Driver, Fuel Tech, Load Tech, Cab Tech.

For v0.2, keep most functional labels unless the user wants named characters. Issue #1 is not yet character-drama first. It is incident-first.

Recommended approach:

```text
Traverse Lead = keep unnamed for now unless later promoted
Rookie Lead Driver = keep unnamed for now, because shame matters more than identity
Rear Driver = keep functional
Fuel Tech = keep functional
Load Tech = keep functional
Cab Tech = keep functional
```

Reason:

```text
Names add reader load before the issue proves its core hazard.
```

If names are desired later, assign them during v0.3 so lettering and balloon tails can remain consistent.

---

## 2. Captions should behave like cold instruments

The strongest captions in v0.1 are short, procedural, and ominous.

Keep:

```text
The approved route was clean.
Shame entered the cab.
Twenty-nine miles can become a country.
The crew came for fuel.
They found proof the road had done this before.
And someone had already decided to do it again.
```

Consider cutting or revising:

```text
Clean flags. Clean weather window. Clean convoy status.
Clean lies best.
```

Reason:

```text
The first page may become stronger if the image and monitor disagreement carry more of the unease.
```

Possible Page 1 v0.2 caption set:

```text
CAPTION 1: The approved route was clean.
CAPTION 2: The flags were cleaner.
CAPTION 3: That was the first problem.
```

---

## 3. Radio voices need clearer texture

The script has solid radio function, but v0.2 should make each radio role sound slightly different.

Suggested voice rules:

```text
Traverse Lead:
short, procedural, no wasted heat

Rookie:
tries to sound calmer than he is

Rear Driver:
practical, skeptical, quickest to spot weirdness

Load Tech:
dry gallows humor, stress through jokes

Fuel Tech:
operational anxiety, protective of procedure

Cab Tech:
systems language, notices network behavior first
```

Use this to revise repeated utility lines so balloons feel less interchangeable.

---

## 4. Lettering load must stay low on action pages

Pages 7 through 10 carry the Jasper encounter and train-risk sequence. Keep balloon count lean there.

Target:

```text
Page 7: visual shock, minimal dialogue
Page 8: alarms and command pressure, not chatter
Page 9: reveal and silence
Page 10: Traverse Lead command clarity
```

Do not over-explain Jasper on these pages. The visual pivot should do the work.

---

## 5. Exterior operation rules are good, but Page 19 needs one extra procedural anchor

Pages 12 and 18 establish that crew only exits for load check and refuel.

Page 19 should remind the artist and reader that the crew are still tethered to procedure:

```text
Fuel lines remain connected.
Crew stay within light cones.
No one breaks perimeter.
Traverse Lead physically blocks one tech from stepping toward the mast.
```

This protects the issue from horror-movie wandering.

---

## 6. The W.A.S. bleedthrough level is correct

Do not increase W.A.S. exposition before Page 22.

Keep as atmosphere:

```text
older cab reflection
obsolete route template
old convoy profile under data
camera-frame human posture under Jasper
route data sediment
scrubbed markings
WORLD APERTURE fragment on Page 20
```

Protect:

```text
No readable W.A.S. label before Page 20 partial fragments.
No Mark Aggressive until Page 22.
No C.A.S.E. in Issue #1.
```

# Page-by-Page Polish Notes

## Page 1

**Works:** Strong cold open. The route disagreement is clear. The single W.A.S. contamination is appropriately subtle.

**Polish:** Reduce captions from three beats of cleanliness to one sharper thread.

**Recommended v0.2 change:**

```text
CAPTION: The approved route was clean.
CAPTION: The flags were cleaner.
CAPTION: That was the first problem.
```

**Risk to avoid:** Do not make the ghost reflection too readable.

---

## Page 2

**Works:** Establishes command structure without making the Traverse Lead incompetent.

**Polish:** Make the SITREP feel less generic by including one detail that matters later, such as route-status confidence or mast network silence.

**Recommended added line:**

```text
TRAVERSE LEAD: Relay grid?
CAB TECH, RADIO: Dead from here to twenty-nine miles. As filed.
```

This quietly prepares the Page 18 relay tower.

---

## Page 3

**Works:** Good first odd-page unease. The old survey drone is a strong Flagstaff seed.

**Polish:** Rear Driver should sound practical, not amazed.

**Possible revised dialogue:**

```text
REAR DRIVER: Side feed caught something.
LOAD TECH: Animal?
REAR DRIVER: Machine. Worse.
```

---

## Page 4

**Works:** Shame as the human hinge is strong.

**Polish:** Keep the rookie's choice silent longer. Let one panel be pure hesitation.

**Recommended note for thumbnails:** Panel 3 should be a close-up of the radio switch with his thumb not pressing it.

---

## Page 5

**Works:** Red light ambiguity lands well.

**Polish:** Do not let the reader assume Jasper is purely bait. The pulse cadence should feel like warning, not lure.

**Recommended visual note:** The red light should appear low and moving wrong, but not obviously attached to a body yet.

---

## Page 6

**Works:** Operational escalation is clear.

**Polish:** Rear Driver's question could become sharper.

**Possible revised dialogue:**

```text
REAR DRIVER: Lead, your line just changed.
ROOKIE: Minor correction around rough ground.
REAR DRIVER: We do not correct unannounced.
```

This raises pressure without breaking procedure.

---

## Page 7

**Works:** Jasper reveal is strong.

**Polish:** Cut Rookie's "What the hell is that?" if the panel itself is doing enough. Keep one reaction balloon at most.

**Possible revision:**

```text
ROOKIE: Contact front!
```

This sounds more procedural and less movie-generic.

---

## Page 8

**Works:** Train danger feels physical.

**Polish:** The alarm text `CONVOY MEMORY LOAD` is evocative, but may be too abstract. Consider a more operational ghost label.

**Possible revised ghost label:**

```text
LEGACY LOAD PROFILE DETECTED
```

Cleaner, more system-like, less poetic.

---

## Page 9

**Works:** Jasper reframed as warning. Cat charm is good, but should be tiny.

**Polish:** Rookie's line "You were stopping us" may explain too much too early.

**Possible revision:**

```text
ROOKIE, whisper: Not chasing.
```

This is smaller and lets the reader finish the thought.

---

## Page 10

**Works:** Traverse Lead finally taking control is strong.

**Polish:** "shut up and listen" is effective but may slightly blunt his professional authority. Use if you want gritty blue-collar command. Replace if you want colder command.

**Possible revision:**

```text
TRAVERSE LEAD: All stations, clear channel.
```

Then:

```text
TRAVERSE LEAD: No reversing. No improvising. Nobody exits.
```

---

## Page 11

**Works:** Controlled halt and silence after alarms.

**Polish:** The ghost-shape reflection is good, but keep it almost deniable.

**Thumbnail note:** Do not center the ghost. It should live in condensation at the edge.

---

## Page 12

**Works:** Exterior operation discipline is protected.

**Polish:** Traverse Lead's final line is excellent and should remain.

**Keep:**

```text
We'll talk after the train can't kill us.
```

This is the issue's best leadership line so far.

---

## Page 13

**Works:** Tool reveal is useful and slightly funny.

**Polish:** "little coffin" is good, but the tone may be too cute for this moment. Could be kept as a pressure valve.

**Possible alternative:**

```text
LOAD TECH: Never thought I'd miss that ugly little thing.
```

The original is stronger if the issue wants gallows humor.

---

## Page 14

**Works:** GPR sequence is clear.

**Polish:** Add one line from Cab Tech to avoid Traverse Lead being the only interpreter.

**Possible added line:**

```text
CAB TECH, RADIO: Those voids are too orderly.
```

---

## Page 15

**Works:** Parallel crevasse reveal is the best structural turn of the issue.

**Polish:** Keep dialogue spare. This page should breathe.

**Keep:**

```text
Safe route's close.
And we can't reach it.
```

---

## Page 16

**Works:** Least-bad decision is clear and competent.

**Polish:** Add fuel pressure urgency to justify tower destination beyond route bridge.

**Possible added line:**

```text
FUEL TECH, RADIO: And we still need the cache if you want heat after midnight.
```

---

## Page 17

**Works:** Good pressure reset. The tiny drone in rear camera is a strong image.

**Polish:** The caption is poetic and good. Keep it if captions remain sparse.

**Keep:**

```text
Twenty-nine miles can become a country.
```

---

## Page 18

**Works:** The relay/fuel cache arrival is cinematic and functional.

**Polish:** Strengthen the rule that refuel is required, not optional.

**Possible added line:**

```text
TRAVERSE LEAD: Fast refuel. No curiosity.
```

---

## Page 19

**Works:** Mast knocks shift the warning language from Jasper to Jack.

**Polish:** Rookie saying "Jasper?" is useful, but make sure the red is not actually Jasper's light. Use fog color/reflection rather than a clear red lamp.

**Recommended visual note:** Red in the fog should come from relay reflection, not a moving headlamp.

---

## Page 20

**Works:** First overt archival bleedthrough. Good restraint.

**Polish:** The fragment `WORLD APERTURE` is acceptable here, but it must not resolve to W.A.S. yet. Keep it partial and noisy.

**Recommended text order:**

```text
WORLD APER...
W.A...
CORE...
PERSONNEL...
```

---

## Page 21

**Works:** Jack reveal is clean. He watches instead of attacks.

**Polish:** Give the artist an exact posture: Jack should stand still while everything else is tense.

**Production note:** Jack's stillness is his power.

---

## Page 22

**Works:** Strong cliffhanger. The W.A.S. reveal pays off the whole issue.

**Polish:** Consider cutting one final caption to reduce over-explaining.

Current:

```text
The crew came for fuel.
They found proof the road had done this before.
And someone had already decided to do it again.
```

Recommended v0.2:

```text
The crew came for fuel.
They found proof the road had done this before.
Someone had already authorized the next mistake.
```

This preserves the dread but lands tighter.
