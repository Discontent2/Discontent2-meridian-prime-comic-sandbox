# Field Crawler Vehicle Sheet
## Mirecat Field Crawler "Mallow" / The Tin Burrow

**Status:** Sandbox Vehicle Design Lock / Ashcan Production Reference  
**Canon Status:** Sandbox only unless promoted through explicit canon review  
**Project:** `Field Guide to Apocryphal Meridian Prime Fauna and Flora`  
**Ashcan Title:** `Observed, Not Owned`  
**Primary Function:** Thor Fortner's mobile shelter, field lab, kitchen, bunk, manuscript vault, and moral-pressure machine  

---

## Core Identity

Thor Fortner's field crawler is the third main presence in the ashcan after Thor and Buckle.

It is not a cool vehicle first.

It is Thor's shelter, laboratory, emotional bunker, tiny kitchen, research den, and little rolling refusal to go home.

Core phrase:

```text
A snow-cat, a survey shack, a food truck, and a tiny cryptid research station all got welded together during a rainstorm.
```


A soft name for an ugly machine. Thor uses it when he is coaxing the crawler to start, apologizing to it, or pretending not to be emotionally attached.

### Common nickname


This is the best project-facing nickname. It suggests shelter, awkwardness, field solitude, and a den-like interior.

This may be what locals call it behind Thor's back.

## Origin / Backstory

The crawler began as a **Ledger Falls timber-margin utility cat**, used near logging boundaries, wet forest roads, snowline routes, and cloud-forest work camps.

It was retired after a track-frame crack, repeated heater failures, and general old-machine disgrace.

Thor bought it cheap, rebuilt it badly but lovingly, and added the hab module using salvaged survey-shack panels, supply-counter scrap, old route-marker hardware, mismatched insulation, and field-lab shelving.

Thematic meaning:

```text
The vehicle was once part of extraction work.
Thor repurposes it for observation work.
```

That scar matters. The crawler carries Ledger Falls history in its frame.

## Exterior Silhouette

The crawler must read instantly in heavy black ink.

Key silhouette markers:

- low tracked chassis
- blunt rounded cab
- front blade like a jaw
- rear boxy hab module
- little stove chimney
- roof rack clutter
- side ladder or fold-down step
- spare track segment bolted to the side
- round headlamps
- rear door where Buckle's pale face can appear

Visual read:

```text
A beetle wearing a cabin.
```

The crawler should be chunky, compact, overburdened, and strangely lovable.

## Front Cab

The cab is Thor's cockpit, rain shelter, and bad-decision throne.

### Exterior details

- rounded windshield
- windshield wipers that smear more than clear
- cage over headlamps
- old warning paint
- scarred front blade
- side mirrors patched with mismatched glass
- roof-mounted marker lamps
- old Ledger Falls permit sticker half-scraped off
- hand-painted `MALLOW` label, possibly with a small marsh-plant icon

### Interior cab details

- steering wheel or twin control sticks
- hand throttle
- route lamp
- heater vent that barely works
- old radio
- hanging charm from Ledger Falls
- glove box full of field slips
- cracked pressure gauge
- tiny clipboard
- coffee tin in cup holder
- glove-box label: `DO NOT STORE SPORES`

Story rule:

```text
The cab is where Thor pretends he is in control.
The hab is where the story proves he is not.
```

## Front Blade

The front blade is visually and thematically important.

### Practical use

- clearing snow
- pushing mud
- shoving flood debris aside
- clearing roots and fallen branches
- scraping mineral crust
- roughing out campsites
- protecting the crawler nose

### Visual role

The blade gives the crawler a face. It should feel like a battered lower jaw, always chewing through terrain.

### Theme role

The blade shows Thor's contradiction:

```text
He wants to observe the world, but even reaching it means disturbing it.
```

Late-story growth option: Thor refuses to push farther with the blade because he realizes doing so would damage a hidden biome.

## Hab as Character

The hab should change with the story.

### Beginning

Organized. Thor has a system.

- neat labels
- carefully stacked notebooks
- manuscript locked away
- clean specimen jars
- Buckle has a temporary holding area only

### Middle

Chaos blooms.

- Buckle hair and mud everywhere
- Gift-Pod jar sweating
- chewed labels
- damp field plates curling
- loose sample tins
- Thor sleeping badly
- Lung Lichen tin glowing at night
- e-bike battery charging in the wrong place

### End

Less organized, but more honest.

- fewer samples
- more drawings
- redacted maps
- `LOCATION WITHHELD` stamps
- Buckle has a permanent nest
- manuscript stored with respect, not obsession
- dangerous specimen storage removed or reduced

Story rule:

```text
The hab is a moral barometer.
```

## Buckle and the Crawler

Buckle believes the crawler belongs to him.

Buckle's crawler behaviors:

- blocks the rear door
- sleeps beside the stove
- chews sample tags
- noses the air vents
- refuses to leave during rain
- knows where the food is
- hides under the desk during storms
- stares at the Gift-Pod jar
- licks condensation from windows
- fogs the glass with his snout
- eventually opens or compromises the feed drawer

Visual rule:

```text
Buckle's pale body inside the dark hab gives strong black-and-white panel compositions.
```

---

## Relationship With Thor

Thor thinks of Mallow as equipment, but treats it like a companion.

He talks to it when starting it or asking too much from it.

Sample Thor lines:

```text
Come on, Mallow. Be ugly and brave.
```

```text
One more ridge. Then I'll fix the heater. I mean it this time.
```

```text
Do not die in front of the animal. It scares him.
```

Character rule:

```text
Thor performs competence in the cab.
Thor admits doubt in the hab.
```

## Relationship With the E-Bike

The e-bike arrives later in the story, at a different location from Buckle's purchase.

The field crawler eventually carries or charges the e-bike, which is likely nicknamed:

```text
Buzz-Mule
```

### Best storage solution

```text
Side-mounted folding rack
```

The e-bike hangs on the passenger side of the hab under a tarp, with a charging cable running through a questionable patched port.

This creates:

- silhouette interest
- easy page visibility
- theft risk
- Buckle sniffing it suspiciously
- a later scene where the rack breaks or jams

## Mechanical Personality

The crawler should have recurring flaws.

Suggested flaws:

- heater works only when parked at an angle
- left track squeaks before storms
- front blade sticks halfway up
- cab fan smells like old moss
- battery meter lies
- rear door latch freezes
- roof leaks over Thor's desk
- stove pipe clogs with fungal dust
- radio catches strange local chatter
- windshield wiper has three moods: useless, frantic, haunted

The vehicle should be lovable and precarious, not slapstick-only.

## Size / Scale

Suggested rough scale:

```text
Length: 18-22 feet including blade
Width: 7-8 feet
Height: 8-9 feet with roof rack / chimney
Interior: cramped standing or crouched standing depending final design
Capacity: sleeps one human badly, plus one Buckle comfortably because Buckle ignores personal space
```

The crawler should be small enough to feel vulnerable and big enough to function as a mobile home.

## Story Functions

The field crawler does five jobs.

### 1. Shelter

Thor survives because the crawler exists.

### 2. Lab

It lets him compare manuscript, samples, notes, and field observations.

### 3. Emotional isolation chamber

When Thor is alone, the crawler becomes his confessional.

### 4. Visual transition device

It carries the story from biome to biome without turning the comic into a Traverse story.

### 5. Moral test

Can Thor enter rare places without turning his machine into a blade, cage, or contamination vector?
