## One-Line Pitch

Thor Fortner, a young field-naturalist from Ledger Falls, follows an ancient Aeonolacertian beast manuscript across rare Meridian Prime biomes to document apocryphal creatures, only to learn that proving a living thing exists can become the first step toward destroying it.

---

## Core Emotional Premise

Thor begins with this belief:

```text
If I document a creature, I save it.
```

By the end, he understands:

```text
If I document it carelessly, I may be the first predator it could not hide from.
```

Final field-guide ethic:

```text
Observed, not owned.
```

## Protagonist

### Thor Fortner

Thor Fortner is a young field-naturalist, illustrator, and stubborn Ledger Falls local who grew up where cloud rainforest becomes cargo and every tree seems to have a permit number before it has a name.

He is not a polished academy explorer. He is a mud-boot kid with a sketchbook, a battered field jacket, too many unpaid fuel tabs, and a deep suspicion that official biology on Meridian Prime is mostly paperwork standing in front of a forest.

Thor sketches animals in permit margins, draws moss in the blank spaces of work orders, and keeps a private notebook full of creatures people insist are extinct, exaggerated, sacred, misclassified, or bad for business.

### Thor's Dramatic Flaw

Thor thinks reverence and pursuit can coexist without conflict.

He believes curiosity is automatically noble.

### Thor's Arc

Thor begins as someone who wants to prove the world wrong.

He ends as someone willing to leave parts of the world unproven so they can survive.

## Core Object

### The Ancient Aeonolacertian Manuscript

Thor obtains a strange ancient Aeonolacertian field work that resembles a Voynich Manuscript type artifact. It contains curling pressure-script, botanical diagrams, saurian marginalia, creature plates, hidden annotations, and biological drawings that seem to mix science, memory, warning, and sacred field practice.

Thor believes it is a key to finding rare creatures.

The manuscript is actually a warning system.

### The Index Orchid

The Index Orchid is a rare black-glass flower whose pollen reveals hidden annotations in the manuscript.

It is both the MacGuffin organism and the method by which Thor unlocks deeper layers of the book.

---

## Supporting Companion

### Buckle

Buckle is a pygmy Pale Burdenback who becomes Thor's beloved but costly travel companion, pet, friend, alarm system, and emotional barometer.

Buckle is a White Elephant in both story structure and daily life.

Buckle eats specialized salt-moss, chews pencils, knocks over specimen drawers, sleeps in the wrong places, bleats when pressure shifts, and repeatedly notices dangers before Thor does.

Buckle should be lovable, inconvenient, and essential.

---

## Main Creature Goal

### The Girasol

The Girasol is Thor's White Whale.

It is an okapi-like sky-island creature with crystalline antlers. At the end of the story, Thor discovers that it seems to glow from within by scattering light through internal structures. The floating internal glow appears to move and track light sources, mimicking the way a sunflower tracks the sun.

This reveal is reserved for the end of the story.

Thor ultimately observes the Girasol but withholds its location.

Final guide entry:

```text
Girasol observed. Location withheld by author.
```

## Synopsis

Thor Fortner lives in Ledger Falls, where the forest is always being counted by someone else. He sketches animals in permit margins, draws moss in the blank spaces of work orders, and keeps a private notebook full of creatures people insist are either extinct, exaggerated, sacred, or bad for business.

When Thor obtains an ancient Aeonolacertian manuscript filled with unreadable diagrams and impossible fauna, he becomes convinced it is the key to a lost ecological truth. The manuscript references the Index Orchid, a rare plant whose pollen can reveal hidden text. If Thor can find the orchid, he believes he can unlock the book and create a new field guide: a record of Meridian Prime's apocryphal fauna and flora.

Thor outfits his battered Sno-Cat-like crawler and leaves Ledger Falls alone.

His first supply stop goes sideways. At a shady fringe port, he buys bad maps, fuel filters, salt-moss, and a beautiful floating Gift-Pod charm from a vendor who knows too much about old field books. He also accidentally adopts a pygmy Pale Burdenback named Buckle, whose care immediately drains his money, space, patience, and emotional defenses.

Thor enters the mangroves chasing Index Orchid clues, but the Gift-Pod begins to awaken in damp heat. He does not realize he is carrying invasive life into rare habitats. Buckle notices first. Thor ignores the warning because he is too focused on proving the manuscript.

In crystal caves outside the Craton, Thor follows the Crimson Glass Salamander, believing it points toward the legendary Girasol. The caves turn oxygen-poor. The salamanders vanish. Thor collapses near glowing Lung Lichen, and Buckle's panic forces him to notice the lichen's light. The Lung Lichen saves him. Thor records his first major correction: the spectacular creature misled him, while the humble organism saved his life.

At the Red Glass sink gardens, the Index Orchid finally blooms. Its pollen reveals hidden annotations in the ancient manuscript. But instead of clear directions, Thor finds contradictions: crossed-out coordinates, warnings not to publish nesting sites, sketches labeled "observed, not located," and notes suggesting the manuscript's author regretted being followed.

Thor continues into the Craton swamp bowels, where he witnesses an ancient dwarf Bajadasaurus-like sauropod. He wants to measure it, prove it, draw every detail. Craton guardians refuse him. At first, Thor sees this as obstruction. Then he watches the animal pass through steaming water, untouched by evolution into personhood, untouched by official categories, sacred because it remained itself. Thor draws it from memory and leaves the location blank.

The journey then turns outward to tropical crystal fjords, where the Nightwake appears. Its black wings prove the existence of a seasonal ecological corridor no official map recognizes. Thor realizes the old manuscript is not a map to rare creatures. It is a map of relationships between rare places.

But the Gift-Pod contamination worsens. Thor's own crawler has become a Trojan Horse. He burns supplies, destroys samples, cuts infected panels from his hab module, and nearly loses Buckle in the process. The expedition becomes less about finding the Girasol and more about becoming worthy of seeing it.

In the final act, Thor reaches the sky islands. He sees tracks, then antler shadows, then movement inside the fog.

At sunrise, the Girasol appears.

It is okapi-like, quiet, long-legged, and striped by shadow. Its crystalline antlers catch the dawn. Then Thor sees the impossible detail: light seems to move inside it. Not a glow like a lamp, but a floating internal radiance scattering through antler, eye, and translucent tissue. The glow slowly shifts, tracking the sun.

Thor has the proof.

He has the location.

He has the drawing.

He also understands what every creature has been teaching him.

He closes the field guide.

In the final published version, the Girasol entry contains the drawing, behavioral notes, and a single line:

```text
Location withheld by author.
```

The last page shows Thor back in his crawler, Buckle asleep beside the stove, the manuscript locked away, and a new title written across the front of his own notebook:

```text
Field Guide to Apocryphal Meridian Prime Fauna and Flora
Volume I: Observed, Not Owned
```

## Hero's Journey Structure

1. Ordinary World: Thor in Ledger Falls, sketching against permit culture.
2. Call to Adventure: the ancient Aeonolacertian manuscript enters his life.
3. Refusal: official channels dismiss the manuscript and the apocryphal creatures.
4. Crossing the Threshold: Thor leaves in the field crawler.
5. Tests and Shady Supply Stops: fringe ports, bad maps, Buckle, Gift-Pod.
6. First Discovery: Index Orchid reveals hidden manuscript layers.
7. Descent into the Strange: rare biomes, cave danger, Craton swamp.
8. Ordeal: Thor becomes the invasive threat through Gift-Pod contamination.
9. Reward: signs of the Girasol become undeniable.
10. Road Back: Thor is followed by collectors, institutions, or his own bad decisions.
11. Resurrection: Thor sees the Girasol and chooses not to expose its location.
12. Return with the Elixir: the guide is published with missing maps and protective absences.
