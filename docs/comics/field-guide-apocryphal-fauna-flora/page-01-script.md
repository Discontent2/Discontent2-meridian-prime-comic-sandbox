# Observed, Not Owned - Page 1 Script

**Project:** Field Guide to Apocryphal Meridian Prime Fauna and Flora  
**Ashcan:** Observed, Not Owned  
**Page:** 1  
**Status:** Sandbox Page Script / Draft v0.1  
**Canon Status:** Sandbox only unless promoted through explicit canon review  
**Script Function:** Establish Thor Fortner, Ledger Falls, the field-guide tone, and the core moral conflict between living things and official classification.

---

## Page 1 Title

**The Forest Becomes Cargo**

---

## Page Function

Page 1 should open inside Thor's ordinary world before the expedition begins.

The page must show that Ledger Falls is not just a town. It is a place where ecology, labor, permit systems, logging, rain, and living things collide every day.

Thor should be introduced as someone who looks down and notices the tiny living thing while everyone else is busy counting the forest.

---

## Page Layout

**Recommended panel count:** 5 panels  
**Page rhythm:** Wide establishing panel, two medium work-culture panels, one intimate discovery panel, one final field-note panel.  
**Lettering pressure:** Light. Let the visuals carry the world.

---

# Comic Script

## Panel 1

**Wide establishing panel.**

Ledger Falls under rain.

A black cloud forest presses against a working border town. Logging cables cut through fog. Worker stilts and mill platforms rise over wet ground. Permit boards, warning signs, hauler lights, and sawdust steam crowd the foreground. Giant trees vanish upward into low cloud.

The scene should feel busy but swallowed by black ink: a human settlement trying to turn a living wall into paperwork.

**CAPTION / THOR FIELD NOTE:**  
Ledger Falls taught me every living thing becomes easier to kill once someone gives it a number.

---

## Panel 2

**Medium panel.**

Workers mark trees with tags, paint, and permit ribbons. A World Works-style permit stamp is visible on a clipboard or tag board, but do not over-focus on official branding. The tags look practical, wet, and ugly.

A tree trunk fills one side of the panel. Its bark is thick with moss, tiny fungi, and rain-beaded insects. A big black permit number hangs from it like an accusation.

**WORKER 1:**  
Tag says it goes.

**WORKER 2:**  
Tag always says something.

---

## Panel 3

**Medium / low-angle panel.**

Thor Fortner is crouched in the mud near the base of an old permit-stamp machine, archive outbuilding, or field counter bolted onto a logging platform.

He is young, muddy, under-rested, and fully focused on a tiny organism growing where nobody else would look: a dark little orchid-like sprout or strange lichen pushing out from a crack in the stamp machine housing.

Behind him, haulers and workers move in silhouette. Thor is nearly invisible to the town, but the page sees him.

Thor holds a small sketchbook, pencil, and field lens.

**THOR, small:**  
Hold still.

**OFF-PANEL WORKER:**  
Fortner, that thing ain't paid to be interesting.

---

## Panel 4

**Close panel.**

Thor's sketchbook page.

He draws the tiny organism. The sketch is careful and beautiful. Around it are rough notes, false starts, little labels, and a partial phrase:

`unlisted growth / permit machine seam / black petal? / pollen unknown`

The living sprout has one microscopic dot of spot color, depending variant:

- Gold if hinting Index Orchid / manuscript destiny
- Green if hinting Lung Lichen / biological warning
- Magenta if hinting alien botanical weirdness
- Rust Red if tying to Ledger Falls permit machinery and warning paint

**CAPTION / THOR FIELD NOTE:**  
The trick is not finding rare life.

---

## Panel 5

**Bottom panel, quieter and darker.**

Thor looks up from the tiny organism toward the cloud forest beyond the mill structures.

In the black tree branches, there is a hidden shape that almost resembles antlers. It must be deniable. The reader may not catch it the first time.

A permit ribbon flaps across the foreground like a redacted line.

The rain thickens. The forest appears to lean closer.

**CAPTION / THOR FIELD NOTE:**  
The trick is noticing what learned to survive being counted wrong.

---

## Page Turn Goal

The reader should turn the page wanting to know:

- what Thor saw in the forest
- why he notices tiny organisms nobody else sees
- what the antler-like shape in the trees means
- why Ledger Falls feels like a place that classifies life before understanding it

---

## Notes for Letterer

Use field-note caption boxes that feel handwritten or typed onto specimen labels, but keep legibility high.

Avoid ornate fantasy lettering.

Thor's captions should feel like private field notes, not heroic narration.

Suggested caption label style:

```text
FORTNER FIELD NOTE / LEDGER FALLS / RAIN SHIFT
```

Use sparingly.

---

## Notes for Continuity

Do not reveal the ancient Aeonolacertian manuscript on Page 1.

Do not introduce Buckle on Page 1.

Do not show the Girasol directly.

The antler shape in the tree branches is visual foreshadowing only.

Thor is still local, still grounded, and still pre-expedition.
