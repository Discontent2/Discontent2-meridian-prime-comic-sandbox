# Observed, Not Owned
## Modern Ashcan Art Direction Sheet

**Project:** Field Guide to Apocryphal Meridian Prime Fauna and Flora  
**Status:** Sandbox Comic Development / Art Direction Seed  
**Format:** Modern ashcan explorer comic  
**Core Look:** Mostly black and white, heavy black inks, selective spot color  
**Genre Feel:** Explorer comic / ecological weird fiction / gothic creature pulp / field-guide adventure  

---

## Visual Motto

```text
The world should feel hand-inked, half-seen, and older than the map.
```

The reader should always feel that the rarest creature is just outside the panel.

---

# 1. Visual North Star

This ashcan should feel like a field journal dropped into a haunted ecosystem.

The agreed visual feel draws from:

- massive black silhouettes
- flat, bold, limited color
- crooked ruined architecture
- winter branches and dead-tree shapes
- strange creatures emerging from dark fields
- red, violet, green, gold, and orange used as shock-color
- heavy graphic contrast rather than soft rendering
- eerie dusk skies
- gothic ruins, organic tentacles, bones, snow, black forests
- chunky ink shapes with expressive linework
- colors that feel printed, not digitally polished

The comic should not look slick. It should look printed, bruised, copied, scanned, weathered, and touched by field grime.

---

# 2. Ink Language

## Heavy Black Ink Rules

Black is not just shadow. Black is structure.

Use black for:

- tree masses
- cave mouths
- crawler undercarriage
- ruined windows
- creature interiors
- panel gutters
- foreground branches
- manuscript voids
- sky-island silhouettes
- Thor's isolation
- unknown ecology

The page should often read clearly even if reduced to black shapes and white shapes.

## Line Quality

Use:

- thick outer contour lines
- rough dry-brush texture
- jittery organic hatching
- cracked stone lines
- scratchy field-sketch marks
- hard cartoon-like silhouettes
- minimal soft shading
- black spotting that swallows detail

Avoid:

- airbrushed rendering
- glossy digital gradients
- over-modeled anatomy
- cinematic realism
- smooth concept-art polish

The line should feel confident, imperfect, and alive.

---

# 3. Color System

The interior is mostly black and white with limited spot color options.

## Option A: Acid Magenta

Use for:

- strange manuscript reactions
- alien botanical interiors
- Hydropolis-adjacent signal weirdness
- poisonous spores
- false beauty
- warning halos around dangerous organisms

Mood:

- synthetic
- strange
- signal-haunted
- uncanny

Best use:

Small accents, not full-page dominance.

---

## Option B: Rust Red

Use for:

- Ledger Falls industrial grime
- old warning paint
- Craton cave minerals
- Red Glass references
- blood-memory without gore
- permit stamps
- old field corrections

Mood:

- earthy
- harsh
- bodily
- industrial

Best use:

Stamp marks, mineral veins, machine scars, old warnings.

---

## Option C: Lichen Green

Use for:

- Lung Lichen
- oxygen warning glow
- hidden life in darkness
- damp caves
- biological safety cues
- spores and moss
- quiet survival

Mood:

- eerie
- medicinal
- organic
- watchful

Best use:

Make green rare enough that when it appears, the reader learns to pay attention.

---

## Option D: Girasol Gold

Use for:

- Index Orchid pollen
- hidden manuscript lines
- Girasol foreshadowing
- crystalline antler glow
- sacred discovery
- final-page wonder
- Thor's most important field annotations

Mood:

- mythic
- precious
- restrained
- luminous

Best use:

Primary ashcan identity color.

---

# 4. Recommended Interior Color Hierarchy

Main interior palette:

- black
- white
- cool gray / blue-gray
- optional violet-gray dusk tone
- Girasol Gold as primary spot color

Secondary optional accents:

- Lichen Green for Lung Lichen scenes
- Rust Red for Craton, warning, and industrial marks
- Acid Magenta for manuscript or signal weirdness

Rule:

```text
Every color must mean something.
```

Do not use color just to decorate.

---

# 5. Page Atmosphere

Backgrounds should be graphic, moody, and readable.

Preferred background types:

- black tree silhouettes against violet-gray sky
- broken arches under snow
- huge dark buildings with glowing windows
- cave walls filled with white biological dots
- snow caps drawn as simple flat shapes
- ruins swallowed by branches
- swamp steam rendered as blank white negative space
- crawler headlights cutting through black foliage
- field-guide diagrams floating over darkness

The environment should often feel like it is watching Thor.

---

# 6. Thor Fortner Visual Direction

Thor is a Ledger Falls field-naturalist, not a superhero.

## Silhouette

- compact field jacket
- hood or cap
- scarf / neck wrap
- mud boots
- satchel
- field notebook
- specimen tubes
- pencil straps
- old goggles
- weatherproof gloves
- practical, slightly mismatched gear

## Body Language

Thor should look:

- curious
- under-rested
- stubborn
- more comfortable crouching near a plant than standing in a crowd
- always carrying too much
- often dwarfed by landscape

## Face / Expression

Thor's expressions should be readable but not overly cute.

Common expressions:

- squinting at tiny organisms
- realizing too late he touched something wrong
- awe he tries to hide
- fatigue
- defensive sarcasm
- guilt after ecological mistakes

Thor should feel young enough to make bad calls, but skilled enough to survive most of them.

---

# 7. Vehicle Direction

## Thor's Field Crawler

The crawler resembles a compact snow-cat / polar utility machine with a blade and rear hab module.

## Visual Identity

- tracked vehicle
- front blade
- rounded cab
- rear living module
- roof rack
- sample poles
- spare filters
- exterior storage boxes
- tiny stove chimney
- scratched windows
- hand-painted field marks
- patched panels
- worn rubber, mud, snow, crystal dust

It should look beloved, ugly, and overburdened.

## Mood

The crawler is not an action vehicle. It is Thor's burrow.

It should feel like:

- kitchen
- bedroom
- laboratory
- blind
- field office
- emergency shelter
- lonely little home with tracks

Interior should be dense with:

- hanging mugs
- field plates
- drying plant samples
- creature sketches
- map scraps
- specimen tins
- pencil shavings
- socks near the heater
- Buckle's bedding
- manuscript wrapped in oilcloth

---

# 8. Buckle Direction

## Pygmy Pale Burdenback

Buckle must read instantly as lovable but inconvenient.

## Shape

- small albino pack-beast
- squat body
- soft pale hide or fur
- sturdy little legs
- big dark eyes
- tiny burdenback hump
- floppy or short rounded features depending final anatomy
- always eating something expensive

## Function

Buckle is the emotional barometer.

Buckle notices:

- bad air
- Gift-Pod danger
- Lung Lichen glow
- approaching creatures
- Thor's emotional collapse

Buckle should never become too cartoonish. Cute, yes. Silly, sometimes. But still an animal from Meridian Prime.

---

# 9. Manuscript Direction

The ancient Aeonolacertian manuscript should look like:

- Voynich-like biological diagrams
- pressure-script
- saurian marginalia
- impossible botany
- creature plates
- fold-out maps
- layered annotations
- hidden ink
- stains from swamp, pollen, and old hands

When activated by Index Orchid pollen, hidden lines should appear in Girasol Gold.

The manuscript should feel both scientific and sacred, but never fully readable.

Visual rule:

```text
The book should look smarter than Thor.
```

---

# 10. Creature Design Principles

Creatures must feel specific, not generic monsters.

Every creature needs:

- a silhouette readable in black
- one strange biological feature
- one behavioral hook
- one ecological role
- one emotional meaning for Thor

## Creature Palette Logic

- Girasol: white, black, gray, gold glow
- Crimson Glass Salamander: black and white body with optional rust red or magenta internal refraction
- Lung Lichen: black cave, pale growths, lichen green glow
- Nightwake: black wings, white eye marks, minimal highlights
- Snow-Dire Leafcat: massive black-and-white silhouette, false predator shape
- Gift-Pod: elegant pale pod with one dangerous color accent
- Dwarf Craton sauropod: huge dark swamp silhouette with white steam and tiny gold or green highlights
- Index Orchid: black-glass petals, gold pollen
- Pale Burdenback: pale body against dark fields

---

# 11. Page Design Language

Use field-guide overlays without cluttering the comic.

## Recurring Graphic Elements

- handwritten notes
- creature silhouettes
- stamped labels
- torn specimen tags
- partial maps
- redacted coordinates
- "Observed, Not Owned" notation
- scale diagrams
- old manuscript fragments
- margin sketches
- page stains
- black gutters that become roots, antlers, or cave cracks

## Label Examples

- OBSERVED
- UNVERIFIED
- DO NOT MAP
- LOCATION WITHHELD
- MISCLASSIFIED
- BREATHABLE UNTIL IT GLOWS
- DO NOT ACCEPT AS GIFT
- NOT A PREDATOR
- SACRED, DO NOT TAG

Lettering should feel hand-done, but still readable.

---

# 12. Panel Style

Preferred panel behavior:

- simple grids broken by creature silhouettes
- large black negative-space panels
- occasional full-page field plates
- heavy black gutters
- inset notebook panels
- panel borders interrupted by branches, roots, antlers, or manuscript diagrams
- silent pages for creature awe

Avoid too many cinematic widescreen panels. This is not a movie storyboard. It is a printed artifact.

---

## Cover Concept

Buckle peers from the hab doorway.

The crawler's blade is planted in snow, moss, and crystal grit.

Behind them, black tree branches form a hidden antler shape.

A single spot-color accent glows in the fog.

OBSERVED, NOT OWNED

# 14. Ashcan Texture

The ashcan should feel like:

- a field notebook found in a wet vehicle
- a punk naturalist pamphlet
- an underground explorer comic
- a printed warning
- a creature catalog that does not trust itself

Production texture ideas:

- rough paper simulation
- slightly misregistered spot color
- heavy ink trapping
- black flood pages
- imperfect halftones
- fake field stamps
- page numbers like specimen tags
- inside cover as equipment checklist
- back cover as partial creature plate

---

# 15. What to Avoid

Avoid:

- clean superhero anatomy
- glossy sci-fi vehicles
- generic monster manual layouts
- overly pretty fantasy creatures
- smooth gradients
- too many colors at once
- realistic wildlife documentary lighting
- lore dumps
- cute mascot overuse
- making Thor look too competent
- making the field guide feel official

The comic should not feel like an encyclopedia.

It should feel like an argument with a living planet.

---

# 16. One-Sentence Art Prompt

A heavy-black-ink modern explorer ashcan comic in mostly black and white with selective spot color, following Thor Fortner and his battered snow-cat field crawler through haunted Meridian Prime biomes, using gothic silhouettes, field-guide annotations, strange fauna, ruined ecology, and luminous rare color accents to make discovery feel dangerous, sacred, and unfinished.

---

# 17. Final Art Rule

```text
Black ink does the storytelling.
Color tells the truth only when the page earns it.
```
