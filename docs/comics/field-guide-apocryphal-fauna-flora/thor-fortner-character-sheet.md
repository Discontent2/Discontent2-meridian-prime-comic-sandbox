# Thor Fortner Character Sheet
## Field Guide to Apocryphal Meridian Prime Fauna and Flora

**Status:** Sandbox Character Development / Ashcan Production Reference  
**Canon Status:** Sandbox only unless promoted through explicit canon review  
**Project:** `Observed, Not Owned`  
**Character:** Thor Fortner  
**Home Region:** Ledger Falls  
**Role:** Young field-naturalist / explorer-romantic / future author of an apocryphal field guide  

---

## Core Character Line

Thor Fortner is a young Ledger Falls field-naturalist who styles himself like a machine-age explorer filtered through muddy local workwear. He wears a dark flat cap with early motoring goggles pushed on top, carries a beloved Kuker-Rankin hand level as a sight glass, and keeps the larger tools of his research life inside his battered hab crawler.

Thor wants to become the kind of explorer old books promised could exist.

Meridian Prime teaches him to become a witness instead.

---

## Story Function

Thor is the point-of-view character for `Field Guide to Apocryphal Meridian Prime Fauna and Flora`.

He begins the story believing:

```text
If I document a creature, I save it.
```

He ends the story understanding:

```text
If I document it carelessly, I may be the first predator it could not hide from.
```

His personal arc is the collapse of explorer vanity into ecological restraint.

---

## Percy Fawcett Influence, Revised Through Ledger Falls

Thor fashions himself after old explorer legends, especially the romantic idea of Percy Fawcett-type expeditionary fieldwork. This influence should show as aspiration, costume, flaw, and private mythmaking.

He should not look like a polished imperial explorer.

He should look like a Ledger Falls kid who built an explorer persona out of old books, repair-shop practicality, inherited tools, machine-age taste, bad weather, and too much sincere ambition.

Best shorthand:

```text
Percy Fawcett dream, Ledger Falls budget, real field talent.
```

## Silhouette Lock

Thor must read instantly in heavy black inks.

Required silhouette elements:

- dark flat cap / driving cap
- early motoring goggles resting on top of the cap
- weathered pocketed field jacket
- scarf or neck cloth
- cross-body satchel or field bag
- high-waisted field trousers
- tall boots or gaiters
- one hand often holding or reaching for the Kuker-Rankin hand level
- lean, restless, crouching field-naturalist posture

The flat cap and goggles replace the earlier pith-helmet idea. This is now the preferred headwear lock.

## Costume Breakdown

### Head

- Dark wool, waxed-canvas, or oilcloth flat cap / driving cap
- Early motoring goggles pushed onto the cap
- Goggles worn down only during wind, grit, spray, spores, glare, or crawler work
- Pencil may be tucked behind ear, into cap band, or into jacket seam

### Upper Body

- Short field jacket with too many pockets
- Dark or muted work shirt beneath
- Scarf / neck cloth used as rag, bandage, lens cloth, or animal tie-off
- Cross-body satchel for field slips, folded notes, light tools, and emergency scraps
- Damp cuffs, mismatched buttons, repairs, weather stains

### Lower Body

- Durable field trousers
- Tall boots or gaiters
- Mud, soot, lichen stains, crystal grit, and rain-darkened fabric

### Color / Ink Read

Thor is mostly black, white, and gray in the ashcan interior.

Spot color should appear only as environmental or narrative contact:

- Girasol Gold: tiny reflection from manuscript, hand-level glass, or final discovery
- Rust Red: Ledger Falls permit stamp, warning paint, or old repair mark
- Lichen Green: Lung Lichen glow reflected on skin or gear
- Acid Magenta: only if Hydropolis / signal weirdness touches him

Do not make Thor colorful. Let the world stain him.

## Removed From Default On-Body Gear

These items should not usually be carried on Thor's body. They live in the hab module unless a scene specifically requires them:

- specimen belt
- old binoculars
- main sketchbook / field plate book

This keeps Thor's walking silhouette cleaner and makes the hab module feel like his true mobile study.

## In the Hab Module by Default

Thor's larger research life lives inside the crawler's rear hab module:

- old binoculars
- main sketchbook / field plate book
- specimen drawers
- sample tins
- camera
- spare notebooks
- larger measuring tools
- preserved samples
- reference materials
- Buckle's bedding and feed
- manuscript storage, usually wrapped in oilcloth

The visual rhythm should be:

```text
Thor steps out light.
Thor returns to the hab to process, sketch, compare, obsess, and make mistakes in ink.
```

## Signature Hand Prop

## Kuker-Rankin Hand Level

Thor refuses to give up his Kuker-Rankin hand level.

He uses it as:

- sight glass
- horizon finder
- terrain reader
- ridge-line checker
- canopy-line tool
- rough surveying instrument
- ritual object of focus

It is not just gear. It is identity.

Thor reaches for it when he wants to impose order on wild terrain, look competent, steady his nerves, or believe the world can still be measured from where he stands.

Symbolic meaning:

```text
Thor begins by believing that if he can sight a thing, measure a thing, or line it up, he can understand it.
```

By the end, he still keeps the hand level, but uses it with humility. He learns that a sight line is not ownership.

## Physical Read

Recommended age:

```text
22 to 26
```

Build:

- lean from field labor
- not bulky
- slightly underfed expedition look
- long-limbed and restless
- crouches often
- always looking at tracks, lichen, roots, vents, or horizon breaks

Face / expression:

- weather-chapped cheeks
- tired eyes
- squint from rain, fog, and bad goggles
- ink, mud, or graphite on fingers and cuffs
- handsome only in the muddy field-journal sense

---

## Body Language

Thor should frequently:

- push goggles up with dirty fingers
- tug the flat cap brim down in rain
- sight through the hand level with intense concentration
- crouch low to inspect lichen, tracks, roots, or spores
- hold the hand level like a tiny ritual telescope
- look mildly offended when nature refuses to match his assumptions
- talk to Buckle like Buckle is a colleague

Recurring visual gag:

```text
Thor peers through the hand level at a distant ridge like he is reading destiny, then immediately steps in mud or gets corrected by Buckle.
```

## Personality

Thor is:

- sincere
- stubborn
- underfunded
- talented
- romantic about fieldwork
- lonely but unwilling to admit it
- precise when nervous
- sarcastic when embarrassed
- more comfortable with plants and animals than people
- dangerous because he thinks curiosity is automatically noble

Core flaw:

```text
Thor believes curiosity is automatically noble.
```

This is the engine of the story.

He is not greedy. He is not cruel. He is bright-eyed and dangerous because he thinks wonder excuses intrusion.

---

## Voice

Thor's speech should mix field-manual precision, Ledger Falls practicality, and private melodrama.

Voice traits:

- precise when nervous
- sarcastic when embarrassed
- poetic mostly in captions or private field notes
- uses field-guide language to avoid feelings
- talks to Buckle as if Buckle is an expedition assistant

Sample dialogue:

```text
I need waterproof paper, clean fuel, and a map that lies less than the last one.
```

```text
That was not a snack. That was a numbered specimen tag.
```

```text
The organism is either reacting to pressure change, mineral light, or my complete failure to understand where I am standing.
```

```text
Good. Excellent. The cave is breathing. That is a normal thing caves do when they are polite.
```

## Relationship to Ledger Falls

Thor comes from the edge between forest and industry.

Best working background:

```text
Thor's family runs or ran a small field supply and repair counter near Ledger Falls, serving loggers, survey crews, conservationists, route workers, and field operators.
```

This gives him:

- machine competence
- local ecology knowledge
- class tension
- supply debt
- access to odd tools
- reason to own or rebuild a strange field crawler
- resentment toward official science
- practical understanding of how living things become paperwork

Ledger Falls taught Thor that naming something can become the first step toward cutting, selling, moving, tagging, or owning it.

---

## Relationship to Buckle

Buckle, the pygmy Pale Burdenback, punctures Thor's explorer fantasy.

Thor tries to be dignified.

Buckle eats the dignity.

Buckle notices what Thor misses:

- bad air
- Gift-Pod danger
- Lung Lichen glow
- approaching creatures
- emotional danger
- when Thor is becoming too much like a collector

Buckle is not merely comic relief. Buckle is the heart-meter.

## Visual Evolution

### Act I Thor

- cleanest version of outfit
- cap shaped and goggles intact
- jacket organized
- hand level polished too often
- trying to look like a proper explorer portrait

### Act II Thor

- jacket torn
- cap rain-warped
- goggles scratched
- pockets overstuffed
- Buckle hair on everything
- notes water-damaged
- hand level still carefully protected

### Act III Thor

- no longer looks like the explorer he imagined
- looks like himself
- cap patched
- scarf stained
- jacket repaired with local cloth or field stitching
- hand level worn but respected
- field guide pages less certain and more honest

Final design principle:

```text
The more Thor becomes himself, the less he looks like the explorer he was imitating.
```
