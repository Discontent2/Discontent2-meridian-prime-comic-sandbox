## Core Summary

The Cairn Hound is the Core-returned remnant of **Mara "Sparky" Cairn**, the mountaineer and vertical route specialist of the first WISSARD traverse to the Core: the **W.A.S. Traverse**.

Mark Aggressive sent himself and his team into the Core before the Core was ready for traveling.

When the team came back, they were no longer only themselves.

They were meshed with their most prominent forgotten memories.

For Mara, that memory was **Massif**, called **Massey**, the dog she left back home.

The result is a tall, gaunt, crystal-grown dogman cryptid in tattered modern mountaineering garb, seen near high cairns, old traverse routes, cave mouths, avalanche paths, and impossible ridgelines.

People call her **Chupacobra** because she looks like something that might drain blood.

She does not.

Mostly, Sparky stays alone in her mountain cave hermitage.

## Mara "Sparky" Cairn

Mara Cairn was the W.A.S. Traverse mountaineer.

Her specialty was vertical route judgment: cliff access, ice lines, exposed ridges, anchor placement, rescue hauling, and route assessment where maps stopped being useful.

The nickname **Sparky** had two layers.

Team meaning:

```text
She could restart dead equipment, improvise heat, coax batteries back from cold, and curse a generator into obedience.
```

Private meaning:

```text
Massey got excited whenever static snapped from Mara's expedition fleece.
To the dog, "Sparky" meant she was home.
```

Mara was not sentimental in the field. She was practical, dry, disciplined, and good at surviving places that wanted people to fall.

That is why the Core did not mesh her with triumph, training, or summit records.

It remembered who waited for her below the mountains.

---

## Massif "Massey"

Massif, called **Massey**, was Mara's dog back home.

Massey was not a monster, weapon, or working guard animal.

Massey was the part of Mara's life that made mountains stop being work.

Core memory:

```text
Mara comes home exhausted, unlacing frozen boots.
Massey presses his head against her knee as if the world has not ended.
```

That memory became the emotional anchor of her returned form.

The Core took the dog at the door and wrapped it around the mountaineer who never made it back properly.

## Current Form

The Cairn Hound is a humanoid dogman cryptid with mountaineer posture and mineralized body growth.

Visual traits:

- tall gaunt dogman silhouette
- intelligent canine face, not a human face
- wolfhound / mountain dog influence rather than werewolf savagery
- long ears, sharp muzzle, reflective amber or pale eyes
- crystal growths along shoulders, back, cheek ruff, forearms, and climbing gear
- translucent blue-white, clear, smoky, or redglass crystal facets
- tattered modern mountaineering jacket and expedition pants
- old harness, straps, rope, carabiners, belay hardware, and crampon scars
- pack straps grown into fur and crystal in places
- hands midway between gloved climber hands and canine paws
- digitigrade legs with climbing strength
- frost, scree dust, and old route grime embedded in fur
- cave-hermit stillness rather than attack posture

She should look frightening but not grotesque for shock value.

She looks like a person, a dog, a mountain route, and a broken memory learned to stand in the same body.

## Garb and Gear

Sparky still wears modern mountaineering garb by present-day standards, but it is ancient by condition.

Garb notes:

- blue-gray or dark technical shell jacket
- insulated climbing layers beneath
- torn snow pants or expedition trousers
- old climbing harness
- worn pack
- rope coils and loose cord
- carabiners, ice screws, pitons, and belay devices
- gloves torn at the fingers or fused into paw-like hands
- boots split by digitigrade change, or crampon-like claw adaptations
- fabric bleached by altitude, UV, ice, and time
- crystal growth breaking through seams, shoulders, sleeves, and straps

Nothing about the design should read fantasy armor.

It is damaged present-day mountaineering equipment slowly losing a fight with memory, weather, and crystal.

## Behavior

Reported behavior:

- avoids settlements
- watches from ridges, cave mouths, and cairns
- howls before avalanche, cornice collapse, or route failure
- drags lost climbers away from exposed ledges by their packs
- leaves crystal pawprints in frost or scree dust
- collects broken carabiners, cracked helmets, and torn rope
- repairs old anchors in ways that confuse climbers
- refuses to enter Prime Ops territory
- reacts violently to WISSARD equipment, markings, drilling gear, or Core-linked instruments
- protects her cave boundary with warning gestures before violence
- sometimes stacks stones to block unsafe routes
- understands names, especially "Mara," "Sparky," "Massif," and "Massey"
- may not speak often; when she does, it should be damaged, sparse, and emotionally loaded

She is not a roaming killer.

She is a hermit with mountain-danger instincts and a body that makes witnesses invent worse stories.

## The Chupacobra Rumor

The rumor-name **Chupacobra** comes from fear, silhouette, and bad distance-reading.

At night, Sparky may look like:

- a long-jawed blood-drinker
- a crystal-spined predator
- a cave demon
- a goat-killer
- a dog-headed climber corpse
- a thing with puncture teeth and no warmth

The rumor is wrong.

She does not drain livestock or people.

The "blood-drinker" story likely comes from:

- red crystal growths
- scavenged emergency dye
- rust stains on old hardware
- animals found dead after avalanche pressure changes
- bite-like punctures from sharp crystals
- Sparky dragging injured bodies by pack straps or clothing
- fear turning rescue evidence into predation evidence

The mountain made her look guilty.

The Core made her lonely.

## Relationship to Cairns

Cairns are central to Sparky's folklore.

Her surname was Cairn before she became the Cairn Hound.

A cairn can be:

- a trail marker
- a grave sign
- a memory pile
- a warning
- a route claim
- a false guide
- a place where stones agree long enough to point somewhere

Sparky stands beside cairns, repairs cairns, knocks over false cairns, and builds new cairns where old maps fail.

Some highland crews say she is not guarding the mountain.

She is guarding the difference between a route marker and a grave.
