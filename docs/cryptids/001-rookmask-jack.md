## Core Concept

Rookmask Jack is a living Meridian Prime folklore cryptid associated with routes, signal failures, warning poles, dead relays, and moments when maps, manifests, or testimony stop agreeing with the ground.

He is not simply a bird monster, a fantasy tengu, or a person in costume. He is a threshold creature: half-understood by Aeonolacertian and Antisapien cultures, claimed fully by neither, and therefore perfect for rumor.

Core thesis:

```text
Rookmask Jack is a living threshold cryptid whose body looks half-saurian, half-signal-haunted, and whose presence means the route knows more than the crew does.
```

Another field framing:

```text
Rookmask Jack is what route crews blame when the warning arrives before the evidence.
```

---

## Species / Origin Direction

Rookmask Jack should be treated as a **living hybrid-adjacent folklore creature**, not a clean laboratory hybrid or formally understood crossbreed.

Best current interpretation:

```text
Rookmask Jack is a real living cryptid species, believed to be hybrid-adjacent because it combines Aeonolacertian-like bodily intelligence with Antisapien-like witness and signal behaviors. Whether that resemblance is ancestry, convergence, or pressure-world contamination is unresolved.
```

This keeps him biologically solid but mythically unstable.

Possible explanations remain open:

- pressure-born divergence
- signal-touched organism
- old route-lineage relic
- misclassified threshold fauna
- folklore wrapped around a real animal intelligence
- living witness-creature created by environmental convergence

Do not overdefine his origin yet.

## Anatomy and Surface Detail

### Body

- Human-sized, but not human-bodied.
- Upright saurian-avian posture.
- Long limbs with slight predatory economy.
- Narrow torso with a scavenger's balance.
- Clawed hands with tool-capable dexterity.
- Gripping clawed feet suited for rocks, towers, ledges, relay masts, and old route poles.
- Optional subtle tail, functioning as counterbalance rather than spectacle.

### Head / Face

The face is the key focal point.

- More saurian than pure bird.
- Hooked caution-beak, sharp but not huge.
- Masklike skull profile.
- Cowl or face wrapping creates an almost plague-mask silhouette.
- Avoid a literal historical plague-doctor mask.
- Avoid a human face with a beak added.
- Eyes should read intelligent, watchful, and unsettling rather than animal-glowing.

### Covering

Rookmask Jack's surface language is a **fiber-plume / mineral-feather strange coat**:

- layered dark featherlike fibers
- shard-like mineral plumage
- subtle crystalline ribs in feather structures
- matte black fibers
- soot-dark plume edges
- glassy feather spines
- mineral shimmer only in restrained accents
- wind-worn, weathered, naturally strange textures

His tattered robes should merge visually with the mineral-feather coat so the viewer is not fully sure where clothing ends and biology begins.

## Gear Lock

### Old Traverse Backpack

The backpack should look old, practical, and route-used:

- faded canvas or cracked synthetic fabric
- rolled blanket or bedroll
- patched straps
- metal buckles
- small hanging tin cup or canister
- scavenged hooks
- route tags
- field cord
- weathering, frost, dust, and grime

It should imply Jack has walked old routes for years, perhaps longer than any current crew understands.

### Crooked Walking Staff

The staff should be:

- irregular and crooked
- practical but ritual-adjacent
- made from deadwood, old pole material, bone-like mineral, or scavenged route debris
- wrapped with cord, scraps, wire, tags, and small found objects
- not a wizard staff
- not a weapon-first spear

The staff reinforces his scavenger-intellectual identity: walker, watcher, collector, witness.

---

## Behavior

Rookmask Jack is not primarily an attacker.

He watches first.

He scavenges second.

He understands more than he should.

Behavioral motifs:

- perches on dead relay masts
- stands beside old route markers
- taps metal before storms, collapses, signal errors, or route failures
- follows convoys at a distance
- appears where a manifest, map, contract, or story is wrong
- imitates static, clicks, wire hum, and broken signal rhythm
- studies crews like a disappointed inspector
- disappears before proof arrives

He may be feared because his presence is associated with trouble, but it remains unclear whether he causes the trouble, predicts it, witnesses it, or feeds on the aftermath.
