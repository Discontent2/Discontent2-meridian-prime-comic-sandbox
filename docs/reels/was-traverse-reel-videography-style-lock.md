## Core Directive

Use this videography style for the remainder of the W.A.S. Traverse reel project unless the user explicitly overrides it.

The reel should feel like a sequence of haunted, living stills: slow enough to linger, atmospheric enough to feel remembered, and saturated enough to feel unreal without becoming glossy or clean.

```text
The camera does not explain the world.
It drifts through remembered evidence.
```

---

## Format Lock

- Vertical 9:16.
- Short-form reel pacing.
- Each scene should behave like a cinematic tableau rather than a fast-cut action shot.
- Shot duration should generally remain in the 5-8 second range.
- Movement should be restrained: slow push-ins, subtle parallax, drifting mist, pulsing lights, faint camera creep.
- Avoid frantic cuts, fast zooms, music-video whip motion, or action trailer pacing.

---

## Visual Language

The studied reference reel uses a dark, saturated, practical-effects-adjacent fantasy / sci-fi look.

Carry forward:

- deep blacks and strong silhouettes
- wet surfaces and reflected colored light
- heavy fog, mist, steam, or cloud layers
- glowing portals, windows, beacons, instrument lights, biological light, and city light as illumination sources
- a strong sense of miniature or practical set atmosphere
- dark blue, violet, cyan, magenta, amber, red, and occasional green
- lonely figures framed by oversized environments
- slow reveals through haze and distance

Avoid:

- clean daylight
- white flashlight wash
- neutral bright fill
- polished modern blockbuster clarity
- flat, evenly-lit AI fantasy poster composition
- overactive camera moves
- readable explanatory text inside shots

---

## Camera Behavior

The style is not static, but it should feel patient.

Preferred camera moves:

- slow push toward the subject
- slight vertical drift through fog
- shallow parallax between foreground, subject, and background
- slow reveal from environmental darkness
- gentle hold on faces, silhouettes, or artifacts
- atmosphere moving more than the camera

Shot grammar:

```text
establish the atmosphere first
let the viewer find the subject
hold long enough for fear to soften into recognition
cut before the scene explains itself
```

---

## Lighting Lock

This videography style confirms the existing no-white-light rule.

Light the reel with:

- violet portal light
- amber windows and practical lamps
- red beacons and route markers
- cyan mineral glow
- magenta storm or instrument light
- green industrial panels or scanner light
- orange industrial heat or camp-machine glow
- reflections in water, ice, wet metal, glass, and frost

Lighting should feel like it comes from things inside the world, not from an external studio source.

```text
The lighting is the narrator.
```

---

## Texture and Finish

Use:

- film grain
- slight softness
- bloom around colored light sources
- dark vignetting or natural falloff
- mist diffusion
- reflective wet ground
- worn practical set texture
- ice, grime, rust, condensation, cables, stone, tarps, and metal

The image should feel like a rediscovered fantasy-sci-fi VHS artifact cleaned just enough to haunt properly.

---

## Emotional Rhythm

The reference reel is lonely rather than loud.

The W.A.S. Traverse reel should maintain this emotional path:

```text
wonder -> isolation -> strange encounter -> unease -> recognition -> tenderness -> unresolved distance
```

Cryptids should feel scary in the moment but remembered fondly afterward.

They are not jump-scare monsters. They are beings future travelers tell stories about after surviving them.

---

## Composition Notes

Strong compositions should include:

- large vertical scale
- foreground silhouettes or objects framing the image
- a small figure against an immense place
- low fog crossing the lower third
- colored reflections leading the eye
- distant structures visible through haze
- one clear emotional subject per shot

Do not overcrowd shots with too many competing focal points. Dense backgrounds are allowed, but the subject must remain readable.

---

## Scene-by-Scene Application

### The Core Opens

Treat as an overwhelming portal tableau. The figures are tiny. The violet fissure is the real subject. Camera can drift forward slightly as the fog breathes.

### The Shelter / Stopped Traverse

Treat as a lonely field outpost. Motion should come from mist, beacon blink, aurora pulse, and warm window flicker. No characters required.

### Rookmask Jack

Slow hold on a silhouette found beside dead infrastructure. Fear should soften into patience.

### Flagstaff / The Flagger

Show the machine as a route-service relic still performing duty. Movement should be slow, mechanical, and minimal.

### Ol' Jasper at the Crossed Route

Red headlamp and crevasse reflections should dominate. Jasper faces the danger, not the viewer. He is a warning, not a predator.

### X in the Ruined WISSARD Bay

Interior should be claustrophobic and wet. Reveal X in fragments. His hands, antennae, hoodie, and ruined equipment matter more than a clean full-body view.

### The Eustatic Devil / Sea Devil Memory

Jewel-box specimen shot. Let the jar glow. The creature should pulse with small bioluminescent detail while the lab remains dark.

### Sparky / The Cairn Hound

High-altitude guardian shot. Vast depth, cold cyan crystal glow, amber cairn lamp. Stillness is the power.

### The Last Crew Picture

The closest thing to warmth in the reel. Treat as a living photograph being taken in poor field conditions, lit by mismatched practical lights.

### Route Approaching Hydropolis

Final distance shot. Hydropolis is visible but not welcoming. The camera should linger on the route and reflections before the city swallows the horizon.
