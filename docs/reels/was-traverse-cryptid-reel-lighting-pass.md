**Version:** Lighting Pass Revision  
**Runtime Target:** about 60 seconds  

---

## Core Reel Rules

- No narration.
- No on-screen text.
- Each shot lingers for roughly 5–8 seconds.
- The reel should feel lonely.
- Cryptids should be portrayed as remembered by future travelers through stories of encounters with them: frightening in the moment, but remembered with strange fondness.
- Include non-W.A.S. Traverse cryptids to keep the viewer from immediately understanding the hidden pattern.
- Include quiet shots where absence matters as much as presence.

---

## Global Lighting Rule

No white light.

No neutral daylight key light.

No clean flashlight wash.

Every shot must be lit by colored source logic only:

- machine glow
- industrial signal light
- reflected mineral light
- buried thermal light
- chemical flare light
- wet-surface reflection
- biological glow
- ethereal or anomalous illumination

Overall look:

- heavy saturation
- strong shadows
- bold silhouettes
- deep blacks
- lonely cinematic darkness

The world itself, the machines, the anomalies, and the remembered presences are what light the story.

# Shot List / Lighting Pass

## 0:00–0:05 — The Core Opens

### Image

A violet fissure burns inside a cavern of black stone and wet crystal. Five silhouettes stand before it, tiny against the living doorway. Their headlamps flicker like insects before a furnace.

### Lighting

This shot is lit almost entirely by the Core fissure itself.

- Primary light: violent saturated violet / magenta fracture light.
- Secondary light: wet crystal reflections echoing purple and indigo across the cavern floor.
- Tiny accent sources: muted amber and red headlamp glows from the five figures.
- No overhead illumination at all.
- The silhouettes should be mostly black, their edges rimmed in violet.
- The cavern walls disappear into black except where the fissure catches wet mineral textures.

### Feel

The Core is not just visible. It is the light source, and everything else is being judged by it.

---

## 0:05–0:11 — The Shelter

### Image

A tiny route shelter glows on an immense frozen plain under a dark sky. Tracks lead toward it through frost and mineral crust. No figure is seen. It feels like the last safe place before the planet changes you.

### Lighting

This is the most restrained shot, but still no white.

- Primary light: dirty amber tungsten glow from the shelter window.
- Secondary light: cold blue-black atmospheric bounce from the ice and mineral ground.
- Very faint environmental fill: desaturated cyan haze from clouded sky, but never bright enough to read as daylight.
- The shelter window throws a weak amber reflection over wet frost and dark mineral crust.
- Everything outside the spill zone drops quickly into blue-black shadow.

### Feel

The shelter looks warm only because the world around it is so dead and dark.

---

## 0:11–0:17 — Rookmask Jack

### Image

Rookmask Jack stands at route edge near a dead relay mast, hood up, crooked staff in hand, old traverse pack on his back. He looks eerie at first glance, like a watcher or omen. The longer the shot lingers, the more he reads as a patient observer rather than an aggressor.

### Lighting

This should feel like a creature found at the edge of signal.

- Primary light: weak dead-relay sodium amber pulse, intermittent and uneven.
- Secondary light: low cobalt fog glow trapped in crystal dust and mist.
- Small tertiary accents: faint red or rust signal blink somewhere on the ruined mast.
- His robe silhouette should be mostly black.
- The beak/cowl and shoulder edges catch narrow bands of dirty amber.
- Eyes can catch a tiny reflective point, not a glow.

### Feel

He is seen the way forgotten infrastructure sees him.

---

## 0:17–0:23 — Flagstaff / The Flagger

### Image

Flagstaff limps across a cold auxiliary route planting a flag. Old yellow-white chassis, tarp hanging, broken antenna, drill arm, route-surveyor head, quiver of flags on his back. He looks lonely and purposeful. The route he is fixing feels wrong.

### Lighting

This shot should make the robot feel like a moving route marker.

- Primary light: aged route-service yellow beacon light from Flagstaff’s own chassis or damaged service lamp.
- Secondary light: dull red marker glow from a single route light or old indicator on his frame.
- Environmental fill: graphite-blue predawn haze.
- The tarp catches muted dirty gold highlights.
- The metal walker body falls into deep shadow underneath.
- The flag in his hand reads as a silhouette shape with a yellow edge line.

### Feel

He is not illuminated by the world. He is a fading part of the world’s old lighting system.

---

## 0:23–0:30 — Ol’ Jasper at the Crossed Route

### Image

A crevasse cuts the road. Crossed black warning flags form an X over the route. Near the edge stands Ol’ Jasper: red headlamp glowing like a false route marker, shaggy drooping fur, traverse junk on his back, little dragged hab behind him in the mist. He looks like a jump scare until you realize he is facing the danger, not the viewer.

### Lighting

This shot is all about misleading safety.

- Primary light: deep route-marker red from Jasper’s headlamp.
- Secondary light: low bruised blue mineral fog.
- Minor reflection: the crossed black flags catch dull crimson edge spill.
- Jasper himself is mostly a shaggy black mass with red rim hits.
- The crevasse mouth remains nearly black, only hinted by red reflection and a slight blue contour.

### Feel

The red light says “follow me” before the silhouette says “don’t.”

---

## 0:30–0:36 — X

### Image

Inside a ruined WISSARD or traverse service bay, X crouches in partial shadow. Black hoodie, gray cargo pants, warped isopod face, humanlike proportions broken by too many arms and strange anatomy. He is terrifying at first glance, but stillness and posture make him feel thoughtful, like someone remembering how to be a person.

### Lighting

This should be the reel’s most oppressive interior.

- Primary light: sick industrial green and dead sodium orange from broken maintenance fixtures.
- Secondary light: deep red standby indicator reflections on wet floor metal.
- Possible accent: weak violet leak from unseen anomalous equipment or flooded conduit.
- X’s body should emerge in fragments:
  - a hood edge
  - a forearm
  - part of the isopod face
  - multiple hands half-seen in shadow
- No full even illumination.
- The scene should feel lit by failing systems, not by lamps.

### Feel

He is discovered in layers, the way you notice a person-shaped mistake in a dark room.

---

## 0:36–0:42 — The Eustatic Devil / Sea Devil Memory

### Image

A cold modern specimen room, map shelf, or archive-lab hybrid. A bright preserved juvenile Eustatic Devil floats in a jar while nearby old sea-monster sketches, canal notes, or map fragments imply its folkloric older names: Sea Devil, Monsoon Devil, Sluice Devil. The creature is beautiful and strange, scientific and legendary at once.

### Lighting

This is a jewel-box shot.

- Primary light: electric aquarium teal and acid orange-red.
- Secondary light: jar-glass refraction glow bending saturated color across shelf edges.
- Small accent sources:
  - deep magenta instrument lights
  - green archival scanner light
  - subtle warm amber label-box glow
- The specimen itself should be outrageously vivid:
  - neon yellow
  - cobalt
  - saturated purple
  - orange horns
- The surrounding lab stays dim so the jar feels almost holy.

### Feel

This is the most beautiful shot in the reel, and therefore one of the strangest.

---

## 0:42–0:48 — Sparky / The Cairn Hound

### Image

High in the mountains, Sparky stands beside a cairn in torn old mountaineering gear stiffened by crystal growth. Storm closes behind her. She looks almost like a dangerous dogman apparition, but her placement and posture suggest she is holding the safe edge of the pass.

### Lighting

This shot should feel like mountain weather lit from within.

- Primary light: cold mineral cyan glow bouncing off surrounding crystal growths and ice.
- Secondary light: low buried ember-amber reflection from a half-hidden climbing lamp, old beacon, or sheltered cave source.
- Optional accent: dim blood-orange reflection caught inside quartz veins in the cairn.
- Sparky’s silhouette should read clearly:
  - canine-mountaineer profile
  - tattered gear
  - crystal ridge lines
- The eyes should not glow unnaturally, but may catch a sharp amber pinprick.

### Feel

She looks like the mountain taught itself how to keep watch.

---

## 0:48–0:55 — The Last Crew Picture

### Image

The W.A.S. team stands together in the moment, taking what will become their last crew picture before they drift apart. X, Jasper, Sparky, and Flagstaff are present. They are not posed heroically. They are just close enough to read as friends, exhausted, altered, and still briefly together. This is the last time they are all in one frame.

### Lighting

This should feel intimate, broken, and unforgettable.

- Primary light: camp-machine practicals:
  - low amber work lamp
  - weak red indicator
  - small green instrument glow
- Secondary light: violet bounce memory-light implied from the Core incident, very subtle.
- Each character should catch different light notes:
  - Jasper: red lamp presence
  - Flagstaff: yellow service glow
  - X: green/orange industrial shadow
  - Sparky: cold crystal cyan
- Group image should not be evenly lit. It should feel like several incompatible light sources barely making a shared memory.

### Feel

It looks like a real picture taken in bad conditions, and later remembered as precious.

---

## 0:55–1:02 — Route Approaching Hydropolis

### Image

A lonely route stretches across black-blue frozen wetland and mineral flats toward distant Hydropolis on the horizon. The city is enormous, hazed, cold, and impossibly far away. No cryptid is visible. Only the destination remains.

### Lighting

This is the ending destination shot, and it should feel unreachable.

- Primary light: Hydropolis itself:
  - distant sodium-orange utility lights
  - faint deep cyan atmospheric bleed
  - tiny red signal pinpricks
- Secondary light: black-blue reflective flats catching the city’s remote glow.
- Sky is not bright, just graphite storm layers with cyan pressure bleed.
- The route in foreground should mostly disappear into silhouette and reflection.
- Hydropolis is readable less by detail than by its impossible pattern of colored habitation light.

### Feel

The city is so far away that its lights feel less like welcome and more like memory.

# Overall Visual Thesis

The reel should feel like it is lit by:

- things that are still running
- things that are half dead
- things that should not glow
- things that remember heat
- things observed through wet surfaces, fog, and mineral reflection

Not by sunlight.

Not by clean moonlight.

Not by white flashlights.

The lighting is the secret narrator.
