## Purpose

This file captures what was learned while generating the still images for the W.A.S. Traverse / Meridian Prime cryptid reel.

The lighting pass establishes the reel's structure. This file records the practical image-generation lessons: what landed, what drifted, what must be protected, and which visual locks matter most for future versions.

Core lesson:

```text
The reel works best when it feels less like a monster showcase and more like future travelers remembering beings they feared in the moment and spoke of fondly later.
```

---

## Reel Spine

Final working sequence:

```text
1. The Core Opens
2. The Shelter / Stopped Traverse
3. Rookmask Jack
4. Flagstaff / The Flagger
5. Ol' Jasper at the Crossed Route
6. X in the Ruined WISSARD Bay
7. The Eustatic Devil / Sea Devil Memory
8. Sparky / The Cairn Hound
9. The Last Crew Picture
10. Route Approaching Hydropolis
```

The sequence should feel lonely and slow, with each image lingering long enough for fear to soften into recognition.

---

## Global Stills Rules

- Vertical 9:16 composition unless specifically testing a wider crop.
- No narration.
- No on-screen text.
- No white light.
- No clean daylight.
- No clean flashlight wash.
- Heavy saturation.
- Strong shadows.
- Bold silhouettes.
- Practical-effects bad 1980s sci-fi movie quality.
- Visible grain, haze, wet surfaces, dirty reflections, fog, grime, and analog imperfection.
- The image should feel handmade, staged, and cinematic rather than clean digital fantasy.

The lighting must come from the world itself:

- machine glow
- signal lamps
- route beacons
- specimen jars
- crystal reflections
- wet ground reflections
- biological glow
- archive instruments
- city haze
- anomalous Core light

## Shot 1 - The Core Opens

### What Worked

The best version used a violet / magenta Core fissure as the dominant light source. The scene reads as a cave or chamber of black stone and wet crystal, with four human figures and Flagstaff standing before the living doorway.

The image works when the expedition is small against the Core.

### Required Continuity

```text
Four people plus Flagstaff.
Not five ordinary people.
Not a crowd.
Not anonymous soldiers.
```

The four people represent the human W.A.S. Traverse members before the incident. Flagstaff is present as the route guide / survey drone.

### Important Correction

The crew should not have bright lights attached to their backs. They are looking into the Core. The Core itself lights them.

Flagstaff should read as a non-human guide unit, not another suited person.

### Lighting Lock

- violent saturated violet / magenta Core light
- wet crystal purple reflections
- small muted amber and red practical glows only where useful
- silhouettes mostly black with violet edges

## Shot 2 - The Shelter / Stopped Traverse

### What Worked

The strongest shelter image used a remote stopped traverse camp: module cluster, low amber window warmth, dead-cold frozen flats, heavy foreground mist, purple auroras, and a CASE-style Quadtrac on the right.

The shot feels like a temporary human pause before Meridian Prime resumes being enormous.

### Required Continuity

- main shelter module
- additional module to the left
- CASE 550 Quadtrac-inspired tracked tractor on the right
- tractor powered on but stopped
- tractor would pull the modules while traveling
- no people visible

### Important Correction

A wrong extra track appeared behind the tractor blade in generated versions. Future image edits should remove only that offending track while preserving the rest of the composition.

### Visual Additions That Landed

- Fata Morgana effect over distant mountains
- purple auroras peeking through heavy clouds
- low heavy foreground mist
- red light on one module mast
- wet ice reflections from amber shelter light

### Lighting Lock

- dirty amber tungsten module windows
- dim red mast beacon
- purple aurora glow through storm cloud breaks
- blue-black environmental darkness
- no white light

## Shot 3 - Rookmask Jack

### What Worked

The Rookmask Jack still landed strongly as a lonely watcher beside dead relay infrastructure.

He should feel frightening for the first second, then patient and sad after the viewer lingers.

### Required Visual Lock

```text
hooded black ragged field robes
birdlike plague-mask cowl
crooked staff
old traverse backpack
scavenged gear
still and watchful posture
mostly silhouette
```

He should not be a normal human traveler or a fantasy plague doctor. He is a post-Core W.A.S. survivor whose body and memory have become route folklore.

### Lighting Lock

- dirty amber relay pulse
- faint rust-red mast blink
- cobalt mineral fog
- amber edge highlights on cowl, shoulders, staff, and pack straps
- one small reflective eye catch, not glowing monster eyes

## Shot 4 - Flagstaff / The Flagger

### Major Lesson

Flagstaff is easy to drift into the wrong form. Some generations made him too small, too clean, too delicate, or too unlike the established visual. Others made him a generic humanoid construction robot.

For this reel, use the accepted Flagstaff direction from the user's selected image reference.

### Required Visual Lock

```text
Flagstaff / The Flagger / The Last Surveyor
old yellow-white route-service survey robot
large weathered field-machine presence
boxy proportional survey head
single amber optic / survey face logic
broken antenna
canvas tarp scraps
quiver of route flags on back
black tattered flags with warning geometry
one drill hand
one flagstaff hand
track-feet / broad mechanical feet
snow and ice crust on chassis
loyal obsolete route-service machine
```

### Foot Correction Note

The accepted Flagstaff image needed a correction to the foot closest to the flag in his hand. The robot's left-side foot from Flagstaff's perspective should protrude enough to read as a stable track-foot / broad foot assembly, not a shortened stump.

### Behavior Lock

He is not attacking. He is working.

He should feel dangerous because he is still faithfully fixing an obsolete route.

### Lighting Lock

- aged route-service yellow beacon glow from his chassis
- dull red marker lights
- graphite-blue frozen route haze
- dirty gold highlights on metal, tarp, flag pole, and frost
- wet ice reflecting red and amber fragments

## Shot 5 - Ol' Jasper at the Crossed Route

### What Worked

The final Jasper still landed very well.

Jasper stands at a broken frozen route where crossed warning flags mark a black crevasse. He looks frightening at first, but he is facing the danger rather than the viewer.

The emotional read is correct:

```text
He did not lure travelers into danger.
He stood where they would stop and notice the danger.
```

### Required Visual Lock

```text
human-sized lynx-eared feline-sasquatch camp cryptid
matted shaggy fur
drooping spike-like clumps
scavenged Traverse harness
old goggles
red headlamp like a false route marker
salvage packs and cables
tiny dragged hab module half-hidden in fog
```

### Lighting Lock

- deep route-marker red from Jasper's headlamp
- bruised-blue mineral fog
- dull crimson edge spill on crossed warning flags
- red reflections on wet ice
- nearly black crevasse mouth

## Shot 6 - X in the Ruined WISSARD Bay

### What Worked

The X image landed when it treated him as a survivor in a ruined room rather than an attacking monster.

He should sit or crouch in partial shadow, holding broken WISSARD equipment as if remembering how to work with his hands.

### Required Visual Lock

```text
X / Isopod_X
formerly Richard Jameson
four-armed upright isopod mutant
two legs
black hoodie
gray cargo pants
isopod face
antennae
mouthparts
segmented shell-like head and torso details
upper arms larger
lower arms smaller
almost human at a distance, utterly wrong up close
```

### Avoid

- gas mask human
- masked soldier
- insect demon
- normal human face
- helmeted scavenger

### Lighting Lock

- sick industrial green from broken maintenance fixtures
- dead sodium orange from failing utility lamps
- deep red standby reflections on wet floor metal
- weak violet leak from unseen anomalous equipment
- no full even illumination

Reveal him in fragments: hood edge, antennae, face plates, hands, shell texture, cargo pants, and old hoodie.

The Eustatic Devil specimen became strongest when treated as a jewel-like preserved storm organism: beautiful first, unsettling second.

The latest successful direction added:

- shorter devil horns
- improved arms and legs
- neon blue-green rings around black spots
- color continuing onto arms and legs
- visible bioluminescence

### Required Visual Lock

```text
juvenile Eustatic Devil
small curled hellbender / olm / nudibranch aquatic cryptid

translucent tail and fin edges

bioluminescent accents
colors continuing onto arms and legs
```

### Lab Context

The surrounding archive-lab should imply science, folklore, and map-warning memory:

- specimen jar
- old sea-monster sketches
- wet canal map fragments
- rusted sluice keys
- preservation tubes
- Hydropolis archive machinery
- no readable text

### Lighting Lock

- electric aquarium teal through jar fluid
- acid orange-red specimen shelf lamps
- deep magenta instrument lights
- green archival scanner glow
- amber label-box glow
- jar-glass refraction over wet metal and paper

## Shot 10 - Route Approaching Hydropolis

### What Worked

The Hydropolis shot improved when pushed toward Chongqing-like cyberpunk density with only four dominant perimeter skyscrapers.

This shot is the final image of the reel.

### Required Hydropolis Lock

```text
Hydropolis should resemble a Chongqing-like cyberpunk megacity
four dominant skyscrapers on the city perimeter
only four perimeter towers should dominate
high-density city mass between them
bridges, elevated roads, stacked urban levels
wet fog, neon, industrial haze, basin scale
```

### Foreground Lock

- no cryptids
- no people
- no vehicles
- lonely route remains
- broken route stakes
- old track marks
- black rocks
- cracked ice
- shallow reflective water
- low mineral fog

### Lighting Lock

- city is the light source
- sodium-orange utility lights
- cyan atmospheric bleed
- tiny red signal pinpricks
- magenta / cyan cyberpunk reflections
- black-blue reflective flats
- storm-dark sky
- no white light

The city should feel visible and immense, but not welcoming.

## Major Continuity Lessons

### 1. The Reel Is Not a Monster Gallery

The best images carry fond fear.

Each cryptid should feel like something a traveler survived encountering, then remembered with complicated warmth.

### 2. W.A.S. Traverse Members Need Visual Protection

The following visual identities must be protected from drift:

```text
X = isopod mutant in hoodie and cargo pants, not gas-mask human.
Flagstaff = established route-service survey machine, not random robot.
Jasper = shaggy lynx-eared camp cryptid with red lamp, not generic sasquatch.
Sparky = crystal dogman mountaineer, not generic werewolf.
Rookmask Jack = bird-cowl route omen, not normal plague doctor.
```

### 3. Non-W.A.S. Cryptids Help the Misdirection

The Eustatic Devil should remain in the reel because it breaks the pattern and makes the viewer think this may simply be a Meridian Prime cryptid reel.

Only later does the W.A.S. Traverse connective tissue emerge.

### 4. Hydropolis Needs Its Own Architecture Lock

For this reel, Hydropolis must not become a generic industrial citadel.

It should read as:

```text
Chongqing-like cyberpunk basin city
four dominant perimeter towers
stacked bridges and layered urban density
wet neon atmosphere
```

### 5. No White Light Is Still the Strongest Rule

The no-white-light rule is what unified the reel.

The scenes should feel lit by:

- systems that still run
- systems that are dying
- creatures that glow
- cities too far away
- old machines that remember their jobs
- the Core itself
